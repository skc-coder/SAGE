1. Sequential Travel — Samsung Coding Test Style

Bengaluru → Hyderabad/Chennai → Mumbai/Pune Question: A person starts in Bengaluru. With probability 0.1 he goes to Hyderabad, 0.9 to Chennai. From Hyderabad: 0.3 to Mumbai, 0.7 to Pune. From Chennai: 0.8 to Mumbai, 0.2 to Pune. (i) P(Pune)? (ii) P(Mumbai)? (iii) Given he's in Mumbai, P(came from Hyderabad)?

Approach: Step 1 — Note the Markov-style simplification used in the lecture: P(M∣H,B)=P(M∣H) — once you know he went to Hyderabad, the earlier “started in Bengaluru” fact adds nothing more. Step 2 — Total probability for Pune and Mumbai.

P(Pune)=0.1(0.7)+0.9(0.2)=0.07+0.18=0.25

P(Mumbai)=0.1(0.3)+0.9(0.8)=0.03+0.72=0.75

Step 3 — Bayes’ theorem for part (iii).

P(H∣M)=P(M)P(H∩M)​=0.750.03​=0.04

Answer: (i) 0.25​, (ii) 0.75​, (iii) 0.04​

2. ISRO 2016 — Private Car / Bus / Metro

ISRO 2016 Question: Choice between private car ( P=0.45) and public transport. Within public transport, bus probability is 0.55 (metro is the remainder). Find probabilities of car, bus, and metro respectively. (a) 0.45, 0.30, 0.25 — (b) 0.45, 0.25, 0.30 — (c) 0.45, 0.55, 0 — (d) 0.45, 0.35, 0.20

Approach: Step 1 — P(public transport)=1−0.45=0.55. Step 2 — Multiply along the tree: bus =0.55×0.55, metro =0.55×0.45.

P(bus)=0.55×0.55=0.3025≈0.30,P(metro)=0.55×0.45=0.2475≈0.25

Answer: (a) 0.45, 0.30, 0.25​

3. GATE CSE 2008 — Aishwarya’s Study Habit (2-State Markov Chain)

GATE CSE 2008 Question: If Aishwarya studies CS on a day, P(studies Math next day)=0.6. If she studies Math, P(studies CS next day)=0.4. Given she studies CS on Monday, find P(studies CS on Wednesday). A. 0.24 — B. 0.36 — C. 0.4 — D. 0.6

Approach: Step 1 — Monday = CS (given). Tuesday: P(M)=0.6, P(CS)=0.4. Step 2 — Branch 1 (Tue=M, prob 0.6): Wed=CS with prob 0.4 → contributes 0.6×0.4=0.24. Step 3 — Branch 2 (Tue=CS, prob 0.4): Wed=CS with prob 0.4 → contributes 0.4×0.4=0.16.

P(Wed=CS)=0.24+0.16=0.4

Answer: (C) 0.4​

4. GATE CSE 2021 — Signal Transmission (H/L)

GATE CSE 2021 Question: Sender transmits H with probability 0.1, L with probability 0.9. Channel: P(HR​∣HS​)=0.3, P(LR​∣HS​)=0.7, P(HR​∣LS​)=0.8, P(LR​∣LS​)=0.2. If the received signal is H, find P(transmitted was H).

Approach: Apply Bayes’ theorem directly.

P(HS​∣HR​)=P(HS​)P(HR​∣HS​)+P(LS​)P(HR​∣LS​)P(HS​)P(HR​∣HS​)​=0.1×0.3+0.9×0.80.1×0.3​=0.750.03​=0.04

Answer: 0.04​

5. GATE IT 2006 — Rain and Temperature

GATE IT 2006 Question: P(rain)=0.6. Given noon temperature ≤25∘C, P(rain)=0.4. Temperature is equally likely to be >25∘C or ≤25∘C. Find P(rain∣>25∘C). A. 0.4 — B. 0.6 — C. 0.8 — D. 0.9

Approach: Step 1 — Let x=P(rain∣>25∘C). Step 2 — Total probability, partitioning by temperature:

P(rain)=P(>25∘C)⋅x+P(≤25∘C)⋅P(rain∣≤25∘C)

0.6=0.5x+0.5(0.4) ⇒ 0.6−0.2=0.5x ⇒ x=0.8

Answer: (C) 0.8​

6. GATE CSE 2018 — Guwahati/Delhi Temperature

GATE CSE 2018 Question: Given the conditional table below for P(D-temp∣G-temp), and P(HG​)=0.2, P(MG​)=0.5, P(LG​)=0.3, find P(HG​∣HD​) (correct to 2 decimals).

HD​MD​LD​
HG​0.400.480.12
MG​0.100.650.25
LG​0.010.500.49

Approach: Step 1 — Total probability for P(HD​).

P(HD​)=0.40(0.2)+0.10(0.5)+0.01(0.3)=0.08+0.05+0.003=0.133

Step 2 — Bayes’ theorem.

P(HG​∣HD​)=P(HD​)P(HG​)P(HD​∣HG​)​=0.1330.08​≈0.60

Answer: 0.60​

7. GATE CSE 2017 — P and Q Applying for a Job

GATE CSE 2017 Question: P(P applies)=1/4. P(P applies∣Q applies)=1/2. P(Q applies∣P applies)=1/3. Find P(Pc∣Qc). A. 4/5 — B. 5/6 — C. 7/8 — D. 11/12

Approach: Step 1 — Get P(P∩Q) via the multiplication rule using the easier direction:

P(P∩Q)=P(Q∣P)⋅P(P)=31​⋅41​=121​

Step 2 — Recover P(Q) using the other conditional:

P(P∣Q)=P(Q)P(P∩Q)​ ⇒ 21​=P(Q)1/12​ ⇒ P(Q)=61​

Step 3 — Inclusion-Exclusion for the union, then De Morgan for the complement.

P(P∪Q)​=P(P)+P(Q)−P(P∩Q)=41​+61​−121​=123+2−1​=124​=31​ P(Pc∩Qc)​=1−P(P∪Q)=32​ P(Qc)​=1−61​=65​​

Step 4 — Final conditional probability.

P(Pc∣Qc)=P(Qc)P(Pc∩Qc)​=5/62/3​=32​×56​=54​

Answer: (A) 4/5​

8. GATE CSE 2009 — Unbalanced Dice

GATE CSE 2009 Question: An unbalanced die has P(odd face)=0.9×P(even face) (totals). Every even face has equal individual probability e. Given P(even∣face>3)=0.75, find P(face>3). A. 0.453 — B. 0.468 — C. 0.485 — D. 0.492

Approach: Step 1 — Total even probability =3e; total odd probability =0.9(3e)=2.7e. Normalization:

3e+2.7e=1 ⇒ e=5.71​

Step 2 — Faces >3 are 4,5,6: two even (4,6, each =e) and one odd (5, unknown probability P(5)).

P(even∣face>3)=2e+P(5)2e​=0.75 ⇒ 2e=1.5e+0.75P(5) ⇒ P(5)=32​e

Step 3 — Combine.

P(face>3)=2e+32​e=38​e=38​×5.71​≈0.468

Answer: (B) 0.468​

9. GATE CSE 2016 — LED Bulb Lifetime

GATE CSE 2016 Question: Equal number of two LED bulb types. P(>100h∣Type 1)=0.7, P(>100h∣Type 2)=0.4. Find P(>100h) for a randomly chosen bulb.

Approach: Equal proportions means P(Type 1)=P(Type 2)=0.5. Apply Total Probability.

P(>100h)=0.5(0.7)+0.5(0.4)=0.35+0.2=0.55

Answer: 0.55​
