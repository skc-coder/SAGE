Ultimate Comprehensive History Cheat Sheet

Exam Target: CDS / UPSC / SSC / Competitive Exams
Coverage: Ancient India, Medieval India, Modern India (Indian National Movement), World History & Art & Culture
Structure: Chronological Timelines, Important Names, Key Events, Treaties, Acts & Reforms, Battles, Plans & Movements

Part 1: Ancient India (Pre-History to ~1200 AD)

1. Pre-Historic Period & Indus Valley Civilisation (IVC)

  • Stone Age Breakdown:
    • Paleolithic (Old Stone Age): Hunters & food gatherers, unpolished tools, Negrito race, Ice Age (Pleistocene). Sites: Bhimbetka (MP), Soan Valley.
    • Mesolithic (Middle Stone Age): Microliths (small stone tools), animal domestication started. Earliest animal domestication evidence: Adamgarh (MP) & Bagor (Rajasthan).
    • Neolithic (New Stone Age): Agriculture, polished tools, settled life, pottery invention. Sites: Burzahom (J&K - pit dwellings), Mehrgarh (earliest agriculture - wheat/barley).
    • Chalcolithic Age: Copper + Stone age. First metal used: Copper.
  • Indus Valley Civilisation (~2500–1750 BC):
    • Nature: Urban, Bronze Age, grid system, burnt bricks, undeciphered pictographic script (Boustrophedon - right-to-left then left-to-right).
    • Major Sites & Excavators:

2. Vedic Period (~1500–500 BC)

  • Rig Vedic (Early Vedic: 1500–1000 BC):
    • Primary region: Sapta Sindhu (Land of 7 Rivers). Main deity: Indra (Purandhara - 250 hymns), Agni (200 hymns), Varuna.
    • Social structure: Egalitarian, women attended Sabha & Samiti. Monogamy prevailed.
    • Battle of Ten Kings (Dasrajna War): On banks of Parushni (Ravi); King Sudas defeated 10 confederate kings.
  • Later Vedic Period (1000–500 BC):
    • Expansion to Gangetic plain. Iron usage (Shyama Ayas).
    • Deities: Prajapati (Creator), Vishnu (Preserver), Rudra (Destroyer) rose to prominence.
    • Rigid caste system (Varna system based on birth); women lost rights to attend assemblies.
  • Vedic Literature & Upavedas:
    • Rig Veda: Oldest religious text; 1028 hymns, 10 Mandalas. 3rd Mandala contains Gayatri Mantra (composed by Viswamitra for Goddess Savitri). 10th Mandala contains Purushasukta (origin of 4 Varnas).
    • Sama Veda: Veda of chants/music (Book of Melodies).
    • Yajur Veda: Sacrificial formulas & rituals (Prose & Verse).
    • Atharva Veda: Charms, spells, magical medicine.
    • Upavedas: Ayurveda (Medicine - Rig Veda), Dhanurveda (Archery/Warfare - Yajur Veda), Gandharvaveda (Music/Art - Sama Veda), Shilpaveda/Arthaveda (Architecture - Atharva Veda).
  • Six Schools of Indian Philosophy (Shad-Darshana):
    1. Sankhya: Kapila (Oldest philosophy, dualism: Purusha & Prakriti)
    2. Yoga: Patanjali
    3. Nyaya: Gautama (Logic & Epistemology)
    4. Vaisheshika: Kanada (Atomic theory)
    5. Mimamsa (East): Jaimini (Vedic rituals)
    6. Vedanta (Uttara Mimamsa): Badarayana (Upanishadic philosophy)

3. Religious Movements: Jainism & Buddhism

Jainism

  • Tirthankaras: 24 Tirthankaras. 1st: Rishabhanatha (Bull), 23rd: Parshvanatha (Snake), 24th: Vardhamana Mahavira (Lion).
  • Mahavira (540–468 BC): Born at Kundagrama (Vaishali), attained Kaivalya (Omniscience) under Sal tree at Rijupalika river; died at Pavapuri.
  • Three Gems (Triratna): Right Faith (Samyak Darshana), Right Knowledge (Samyak Jnana), Right Conduct (Samyak Charitra).
  • Five Vows (Pancha Mahavratas): Ahimsa, Satya, Asteya (non-stealing), Aparigraha (non-possession), Brahmacharya (added by Mahavira).
  • Sects: Svetambaras (White-clad, led by Sthulabhadra) & Digambaras (Sky-clad/Naked, led by Bhadrabahu).
  • Councils:
    1. 1st Council: Pataliputra (~300 BC), Chaired by Sthulabhadra (Compilation of 12 Angas).
    2. 2nd Council: Vallabhi (512 AD), Chaired by Devardhi Kshamasramana (Final compilation of Jain canons).

Buddhism

  • Gautama Buddha (563–483 BC): Born at Lumbini (Nepal), Left home (Mahabhinishkramana) at 29, Attained Enlightenment (Nirvana) under Pipal tree at Bodh Gaya (Falgu/Niranjana river), First Sermon (Dharmachakrapravartana) at Sarnath (Deer Park), Passed away (Mahaparinirvana) at Kushinagar.
  • Four Noble Truths (Arya Satyas):
    1. World is full of sorrow (Sabbam Dukham).
    2. Cause of sorrow is desire (Dwadash Nidana / Pratityasamutpada).
    3. Sorrow can be prevented by killing desire.
    4. Path to liberation is Eightfold Path (Ashtangika Marga).

4. Mahajanapadas & Ancient Dynasties

  • 16 Mahajanapadas: Mentioned in Buddhist text Anguttara Nikaya and Jain text Bhagavati Sutra.
    • Magadha (Capital: Rajgriha/Pataliputra) emerged as most powerful.
  • Magadhan Dynasties:
    1. Haryanka Dynasty:
      • Bimbisara: Founder; introduced matrimonial alliances (Chellana, Koshala Devi). Capital at Rajgriha.
      • Ajatashatru: Killed father Bimbisara; built Rajgriha fort; patronised 1st Buddhist council.
      • Udayin: Founded new capital Pataliputra at confluence of Ganga & Son rivers.
    2. Shishunaga Dynasty: Founder Shishunaga destroyed Avanti’s power; Kalasoka held 2nd Council.
    3. Nanda Dynasty: Founder Mahapadma Nanda (Ekarat, Ugrasena - destroyed Kshatriyas). Last ruler: Dhana Nanda (overthrown by Chandragupta Maurya with Chanakya’s help).
  • Foreign Invasions:
    • Persian: Cyrus (first invasion), Darius I (annexed Punjab/Sindh).
    • Greek: Alexander the Great (326 BC) invaded India; fought Battle of Hydaspes (Jhelum) against King Porus.

5. Mauryan Empire (322–185 BC)

  • Chandragupta Maurya (322–298 BC): Overthrew Dhana Nanda. Defeated Seleucus Nicator (305 BC); Seleucus sent ambassador Megasthenes (wrote Indika). Adopted Jainism, starved to death (Sallekhana) at Shravanabelagola with Bhadrabahu.
  • Bindusara (298–273 BC): Known as Amitraghata (Slayer of foes). Syrian king Antiochus I sent ambassador Deimachus.
  • Ashoka (269–232 BC):
    • Kalinga War (261 BC / 13th Rock Edict): Changed Ashoka from Chandashoka to Dharmashoka. Converted to Buddhism by Upagupta.
    • Edicts & Inscriptions: Written in Prakrit (Brahmi & Kharosthi scripts) and Greek/Aramaic. Deciphered by James Prinsep in 1837.
      • Major Rock Edict XIII: Mentions Kalinga War and 5 Greek kings.
      • Rummindei Pillar: Mentions tax exemption in Lumbini (Buddha’s birthplace).
      • Sarnath Lion Capital: Adopted as National Emblem of India.

6. Post-Mauryan & Sangam Age

  • Sunga Dynasty (185–73 BC): Founder Pushyamitra Sunga (assassinated last Maurya Brihadratha). Performed 2 Ashvamedha sacrifices (priest: Patanjali). Heliodorus pillar at Vidisha erected during Bhagabhadra’s reign.
  • Kanva Dynasty: Founded by Vasudeva.
  • Satavahana Dynasty: Founded by Simuka. Prominent ruler: Gautamiputra Satakarni (Nasik inscription of Gautami Balasri). Issued lead coins.
  • Foreign Rulers:
    • Indo-Greeks: Menander (Milinda) converted to Buddhism by Nagasena (dialogue in Milindapanho). First to issue gold coins in India.
    • Sakas: Rudradaman I repaired Sudarshana Lake in Kathiawar (Junagarh Rock Inscription - first long inscription in chaste Sanskrit).
    • Kushans: Kanishka (78 AD) started Saka Era (78 AD). Capital at Purushapura (Peshawar). Patronised Vasumitra, Asvaghosa (Buddhacharita), Charaka (Charaka Samhita), Nagarjuna.
  • Sangam Age (South India):
    • 3 Kingdoms: Cholas (Capital: Uraiyur/Puhar, Emblem: Tiger), Cheras (Capital: Vanji, Emblem: Bow), Pandyas (Capital: Madurai, Emblem: Fish).
    • Sangam Assemblies held at Madurai under Pandya patronage. Epics: Silappadikaram (Elango Adigal), Manimekalai (Sittalai Sattanar).

7. Gupta Empire (320–550 AD) - Golden Age of India

  • Chandragupta I (320–335 AD): Started Gupta Era (320 AD); married Lichchhavi princess Kumaradevi; assumed title Maharajadhiraja.
  • Samudragupta (335–380 AD): Called “Napoleon of India” by V.A. Smith. Allahabad Pillar Inscription (Prayag Prashasti) composed by court poet Harisena. Played Veena on coins.
  • Chandragupta II “Vikramaditya” (380–415 AD): Defeated Sakas (Sakari). Chinese pilgrim Faxian (Fa-Hien) visited India. Court adorned by Navratnas (Kalidasa, Varahamihira, Amarasimha, Dhanvantari, etc.).
  • Kumaragupta I (415–455 AD): Founded Nalanda University.
  • Skandagupta (455–467 AD): Repelled the Huna invasion. Repaired Sudarshana lake.

8. Post-Gupta Era & Harshavardhana

  • Pushyabhuti / Vardhana Dynasty:
    • Harshavardhana (606–647 AD): Capital shift from Thanesar to Kannauj. Authored 3 plays: Ratnavali, Priyadarsika, Nagananda. Court poet Banabhatta wrote Harshacharita & Kadambari.
    • Chinese traveler Xuanzang (Hieun Tsang) visited during Harsha’s reign; attended Assembly at Kannauj and Prayag Maha Moksha Parishad.
    • Defeated at Narmada river by Chalukya king Pulakeshin II (Aihole Inscription by Ravikirti).

Part 2: Medieval India (750–1707 AD)

1. Tripartite Struggle & Early Medieval Dynasties

  • Tripartite Struggle for Kannauj: Fought between Gurjara-Pratiharas (Nagabhata I, Mihir Bhoja), Palas (Dharmapala - founded Vikramashila University), and Rashtrakutas (Dantidurga - founder; Krishna I built Kailash Temple at Ellora).
  • Chola Empire (Imperial Cholas):
    • Founder: Vijayalaya (Capital: Tanjore).
    • Rajaraja I: Built Brihadeshwara / Rajarajeswara Temple at Tanjore. Conquered northern Sri Lanka.
    • Rajendra I: Conquered full Sri Lanka & Srivijaya (Southeast Asia). Assumed title Gangaikondachola; built new capital Gangaikondacholapuram.
    • Chola Administration: Famous for autonomous Village Local Self-Government (Uttiramerur Inscription of Parantaka I).

2. Muslim Invasions & Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526 AD)

  • Early Arab & Turkish Invasions:
    • Muhammad bin Qasim (712 AD): Arab conquest of Sindh; defeated King Dahir.
    • Mahmud of Ghazni: 17 raids into India (1000–1027 AD). Sacked Somnath Temple (1026 AD). Al-Biruni (Tahqiq-i-Hind), Firdausi (Shahnama) visited.
    • Muhammad Ghori:
      • 1st Battle of Tarain (1191): Prithviraj Chauhan defeated Ghori.
      • 2nd Battle of Tarain (1192): Ghori defeated Prithviraj Chauhan (established Muslim rule in India).
      • Battle of Chandawar (1194): Ghori defeated Jaichand of Kannauj.

Five Dynasties of Delhi Sultanate

Slave Dynasty (1206-90) ➔ Khilji (1290-1320) ➔ Tughlaq (1320-1414) ➔ Sayyid (1414-51) ➔ Lodi (1451-1526)
  1. Slave / Mamluk Dynasty (1206–1290):
    • Qutb-ud-din Aibak: Founder; called Lakhbaksh (Giver of lakhs). Started construction of Qutub Minar (dedicated to Sufi saint Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki). Built Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque.
    • Iltutmish: Real founder of Sultanate. Introduced Iqta system, Silver Tanka & Copper Jital. Formed Turkan-i-Chahalgani (Group of 40 nobles). Completed Qutub Minar.
    • Razia Sultana: First & only woman ruler of Delhi Sultanate.
    • Ghiyas-ud-din Balban: Destroyed Chahalgani. Introduced Blood and Iron Policy, court etiquette Sijda & Paibos, and Persian festival Navroz.
  2. Khilji Dynasty (1290–1320):
    • Jalaluddin Khilji: Founder.
    • Alauddin Khilji: Imperial expansion (South campaigns by Malik Kafur).
      • Reforms: Branding of horses (Dagh), descriptive roll of soldiers (Chehra), cash salaries.
      • Market Control System: Created Shahna-i-Mandi & Diwan-i-Riyasat.
      • Built: Alai Darwaza, Siri Fort, Hauz Khas.
  3. Tughlaq Dynasty (1320–1414):
    • Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq: Founder; built Tughlaqabad.
    • Muhammad bin Tughlaq (MBT): Mix of Opposites. 5 controversial projects:
      1. Transfer of Capital from Delhi to Devagiri (Daulatabad).
      2. Introduction of Token Currency (Brass/Copper coins).
      3. Taxation in Doab region during famine.
      4. Khurasan Expedition.
      5. Qarachil Expedition.
      • Moroccan traveler Ibn Battuta visited (wrote Rihla); appointed as Kazi of Delhi.
    • Firoz Shah Tughlaq: Built canals (Yamuna & Sutlej). Established departments: Diwan-i-Khairat (Charity), Diwan-i-Bundagan (Slaves), Dar-ul-Shifa (Hospital). Imposed Jizya on Brahmins.
    • Timur’s Invasion (1398 AD): Invaded Delhi during reign of Nasiruddin Mahmud Tughlaq.
  4. Sayyid Dynasty (1414–1451): Founded by Khizr Khan (Timur’s nominee).
  5. Lodi Dynasty (1451–1526) - First Afghan Dynasty:
    • Bahlul Lodi: Founder.
    • Sikandar Lodi: Founded Agra city (1504); shifted capital to Agra (1506). Introduced measurement scale Gaj-i-Sikandari.
    • Ibrahim Lodi: Last ruler; defeated by Babur in First Battle of Panipat (1526).

3. Vijayanagara & Bahmani Empires

Vijayanagara Empire (1336–1646 AD)

  • Founded by Harihara I & Bukka I (1336) on banks of Tungabhadra river (Capital: Hampi).
  • Dynasties: Sangama ➔ Saluva ➔ Tuluva ➔ Aravidu.
  • Krishnadevaraya (1509–1529 AD) - Tuluva Dynasty:
    • Greatest ruler. Assumed title Andhra Bhoja, Abhinava Bhoja.
    • Court adorned by Ashtadiggajas (8 Telugu poets, led by Allasani Peddana & Tenali Ramakrishna).
    • Authored: Amuktamalyada (Telugu), Jambavati Kalyanam (Sanskrit).
    • Built: Hazara Rama Temple & Vittalaswami Temple at Hampi.
  • Battle of Talikota / Rakshasi-Tangadi (1565 AD): Vijayanagara army under Aliya Rama Raya defeated by combined forces of Deccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Ahmednagar, Golconda, Bidar).

Bahmani Kingdom (1347–1527 AD)

  • Founded by Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah (Hasan Gangu). Capital: Gulbarga, later Bidar.
  • Split into 5 Deccan Sultanates: Adil Shahi of Bijapur (Gol Gumbaz built by Mohammed Adil Shah), Nizam Shahi of Ahmednagar, Qutb Shahi of Golconda (Charminar built by Quli Qutb Shah), Imad Shahi of Berar, Barid Shahi of Bidar.

4. Bhakti & Sufi Movements

  • Bhakti Movement:
    • Nayanars (63 Shaivite saints) & Alvars (12 Vaishnavite saints) in South India.
    • Ramanuja: Vishishtadvaita (Qualified Monism).
    • Shankaracharya: Advaita (Non-dualism); established 4 Mathas (Badrinath, Puri, Dwaraka, Sringeri).
    • Basavanna: Founded Virashaiva / Lingayat movement in Karnataka.
    • Kabir: Disciple of Ramananda; composed Dohas / Bijak.
    • Guru Nanak: Founded Sikhism; introduced Langar (free kitchen).
    • Mirabai: Devotee of Lord Krishna in Braj/Rajasthani lyrics.
    • Chaitanya Mahaprabhu: Gaudiya Vaishnavism in Bengal (Kirtan).
  • Sufi Orders (Silsilas):
    • Chisti Silsila: Founded in India by Khwaja Moinuddin Chisti (Dargah at Ajmer). Other saints: Baba Farid, Nizamuddin Auliya, Nasiruddin Chirag-i-Delhi.
    • Suhrawardi Silsila: Founded by Bahauddin Zakariya.
    • Naqshbandi Silsila: Sheikh Baqi Billah & Ahmad Sirhindi (orthodox).

5. Mughal Empire (1526–1707 AD)

Chronology of Great Mughals

Babur (1526-30) ➔ Humayun (1530-40, 1555-56) ➔ Akbar (1556-1605) ➔ Jahangir (1605-27) ➔ Shah Jahan (1627-58) ➔ Aurangzeb (1658-1707)
  1. Babur (1526–1530):
    • 1st Battle of Panipat (1526): Defeated Ibrahim Lodi (used Tulughma strategy & gunpowder/cannons under Ustad Ali & Mustafa).
    • Battle of Khanwa (1527): Defeated Rana Sanga of Mewar (assumed title Ghazi).
    • Battle of Chanderi (1528): Defeated Medini Rai.
    • Battle of Ghagra (1529): Defeated Mahmud Lodi.
    • Authored autobiography Tuzuk-i-Baburi (Baburnama) in Turkish.
  2. Humayun (1530–40, 1555–56):
    • Battles against Sher Shah Suri: Battle of Chausa (1539) & Battle of Kannauj/Bilgram (1540) (Humayun fled India).
    • Restored power in 1555; died falling from library stairs at Dinpanah (Delhi).
  3. Sur Empire / Afghan Interlude (1540–1555):
    • Sher Shah Suri: Built Grand Trunk Road (GT Road) from Chittagong to Kabul. Introduced Rupiya (silver coin) & Paisa (copper coin). Land measurement system (Zabt / Patta & Qabuliyat). Built Purana Qila (Delhi).
  4. Akbar the Great (1556–1605):
    • 2nd Battle of Panipat (1556): Bairam Khan (Akbar’s regent) defeated Hemu (Hemchandra Vikramaditya).
    • Battle of Haldighati (1576): Raja Man Singh defeated Maharana Pratap of Mewar.
    • Reforms & Initiatives:
      • Abolished Jizya tax (1564) & Pilgrimage tax (1563).
      • Built Ibadat Khana (House of Worship) at Fatehpur Sikri (1575).
      • Promulgated Din-i-Ilahi (1582) - universal religion (Birbal was sole Hindu follower).
      • Mansabdari System: Rank system (Zat = personal rank, Sawar = cavalry number).
      • Dahsala System: Land revenue system introduced by Finance Minister Raja Todar Mal.
      • Built: Fatehpur Sikri, Buland Darwaza (commemorating Gujarat victory), Agra Fort.
  5. Jahangir (1605–1627):
    • Executed 5th Sikh Guru, Guru Arjan Dev.
    • Famous for chain of justice (Zanjir-i-Adl).
    • Golden Age of Mughal Painting (Ustad Mansur, Abul Hasan).
    • Foreign Visitors: Captain William Hawkins (1608) & Sir Thomas Roe (1615) from King James I of England.
  6. Shah Jahan (1627–1658):
    • Golden Age of Mughal Architecture.
    • Built: Taj Mahal (Agra), Red Fort (Delhi), Jama Masjid (Delhi), Peacock Throne (Takht-i-Taus).
    • Deposed & imprisoned at Agra Fort by son Aurangzeb.
  7. Aurangzeb “Alamgir” (1658–1707):
    • Re-imposed Jizya tax (1679). Executed 9th Sikh Guru, Guru Tegh Bahadur (1675).
    • Annexed Bijapur (1686) & Golconda (1687).
    • Called Zinda Pir (Living Saint). Built Bibi Ka Maqbara at Aurangabad.

6. The Maratha Empire

  • Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj (1627–1680):
    • Killed Afzal Khan (1659); wounded Shaista Khan (1663).
    • Treaty of Purandar (1665): Signed between Shivaji and Raja Jai Singh of Amber (representing Mughals).
    • Coronation at Raigad Fort (1674); assumed title Chhatrapati.
    • Administration - Ashtapradhan (Council of 8 Ministers):
      1. Peshwa: Prime Minister / General admin
      2. Amatya / Majumdar: Finance Minister
      3. Sachiv / Shurnavis: Royal correspondence
      4. Mantri / Waqia-Navis: Interior / Intelligence
      5. Senapati / Sar-i-Naubat: Commander-in-Chief
      6. Sumant / Dabir: Foreign Affairs
      7. Nyayadhish: Chief Justice
      8. Panditrao: High Priest / Charities
    • Revenue Taxes: Chauth (1/4th land revenue paid for protection) & Sardeshmukhi (additional 10% levy).
  • Peshwa Era (1713–1818):
    • Balaji Vishwanath: First Peshwa.
    • Baji Rao I (1720–1740): Propagated Hindu Pad Padshahi; expanded Maratha empire northwards.
    • Balaji Baji Rao (Nana Saheb):
      • 3rd Battle of Panipat (1761): Marathas (under Sadashivrao Bhau) defeated by Ahmad Shah Abdali (Afghan ruler). Broken Maratha dominance.

Part 3: Modern India & Indian National Movement (1707–1947 AD)

1. Advent of Europeans in India

Order of Arrival: Portuguese (1498) ➔ Dutch (1602) ➔ English (1600/1608) ➔ Danish (1616) ➔ French (1664)
Order of Departure: Danish ➔ Dutch ➔ French ➔ English (1947) ➔ Portuguese (1961)
  • Portuguese: Vasco da Gama reached Calicut in 1498 (welcomed by Zamorin). Francisco de Almeida introduced Blue Water Policy. Alfonso de Albuquerque captured Goa from Bijapur (1510).
  • English East India Company: Formed 31 Dec 1600. First factory at Surat (1613). Golden Farman from Sultan of Golconda (1632). Royal Farman by Farrukhsiyar (1717) (Magna Carta of EEIC).
  • French: First factory at Surat (1668) by Francois Caron. Governor Dupleix initiated proxy wars in Carnatic.
  • Carnatic Wars (Anglo-French Rivalry):
    • 1st Carnatic War (1746–48): Ended with Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle.
    • 2nd Carnatic War (1749–54): Ended with Treaty of Pondicherry.
    • 3rd Carnatic War (1758–63): Battle of Wandiwash (1760) - British General Eyre Coote decisively defeated French General Lally. Ended with Treaty of Paris.

2. British Expansion & Key Battles

  • Battle of Plassey (23 June 1757): Robert Clive defeated Siraj-ud-Daulah (Nawab of Bengal) due to treachery of Mir Jafar. Established British political foothold.
  • Battle of Buxar (22 Oct 1764): Hector Munro defeated combined forces of Mir Qasim (Bengal), Shuja-ud-Daulah (Awadh), and Shah Alam II (Mughal Emperor).
  • Treaty of Allahabad (1765): Signed by Clive; EEIC obtained Diwani Rights (tax collection) of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa. Clive introduced Dual Government in Bengal (1765–72).
  • Anglo-Mysore Wars:
    • 1st War (1767–69): Haidar Ali defeated British; Treaty of Madras.
    • 2nd War (1780–84): Haidar Ali died (1782); Treaty of Mangalore.
    • 3rd War (1790–92): Tipu Sultan defeated; Treaty of Seringapatam.
    • 4th War (1799): Tipu Sultan died defending Seringapatam; Wellesley annexed Mysore.
  • Anglo-Maratha Wars:
    • 1st War (1775–82): Ended with Treaty of Salbai (1782).
    • 2nd War (1803–05): Peshwa Baji Rao II signed Treaty of Bassein (1802) (accepted Subsidiary Alliance).
    • 3rd War (1817–18): Peshwaship abolished; Maratha confederacy dissolved.

3. Governors-General & Viceroys of India

Governors-General of Bengal

  1. Warren Hastings (1773–1785):
    • Regulating Act of 1773 (Created Supreme Court at Calcutta - 1774).
    • Abolished Dual Government (1772). Founded Asiatic Society of Bengal with Sir William Jones (1784).
  2. Lord Cornwallis (1786–1793) - Father of Civil Services in India:
    • Introduced Permanent Settlement of Bengal (1793) (Zamindari System).
    • Introduced Cornwallis Code (separation of revenue & judicial admin).
  3. Lord Wellesley (1798–1805):
    • Introduced Subsidiary Alliance System (First to accept: Nizam of Hyderabad - 1798, followed by Mysore, Tanjore, Awadh, Peshwa).
  4. Lord Hastings (1813–1823): Anglo-Nepal War & Treaty of Sagauli (1816). Suppression of Pindaris.

Governors-General of India

  1. Lord William Bentinck (1828–1835) - First Governor-General of India:
    • Charter Act of 1833 created Governor-General of India.
    • Abolition of Sati (1829) (supported by Raja Ram Mohan Roy - Regulation XVII).
    • Suppression of Thuggee (by Colonel Sleeman - 1830). Macaulay’s Minute on English Education (1835).
  2. Lord Dalhousie (1848–1856):
    • Introduced Doctrine of Lapse (Annexed states: Satara - 1848, Jaitpur/Sambalpur - 1849, Udaipur - 1852, Jhansi - 1853, Nagpur - 1854; Awadh annexed on grounds of misgovernance - 1856).
    • First Railway line from Bombay to Thane (1853). Electric Telegraph line (Calcutta to Agra).
    • Wood’s Despatch (1854) (Magna Carta of English Education in India). Widow Remarriage Act (1856).

Viceroys of India (Post-1857)

  1. Lord Canning (1856–1862) - First Viceroy of India:
    • Government of India Act 1858 (ended EEIC rule; created Secretary of State for India).
    • Revolt of 1857 occurred during his tenure. Indian Councils Act 1861.
  2. Lord Lytton (1876–1880):
    • Organised Delhi Durbar (1877) during severe famine.
    • Passed Vernacular Press Act (1878) (Gagging Act) & Arms Act (1878).
  3. Lord Ripon (1880–1884) - Good Viceroy / Father of Local Self-Government:
    • Repealed Vernacular Press Act (1882).
    • Passed First Factory Act (1881). Hunter Commission on Education (1882).
    • Ilbert Bill Controversy (1883) (proposed trial of Europeans by Indian magistrates).
  4. Lord Curzon (1899–1905):
    • Partition of Bengal (1905). Passed Indian Universities Act 1904. Ancient Monuments Preservation Act 1904.
  5. Lord Hardinge II (1910–1916):
    • Annulled Partition of Bengal (1911). Shifted capital from Calcutta to Delhi (1911).
  6. Lord Chelmsford (1916–1921): Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (GOI Act 1919), Rowlatt Act (1919), Jallianwala Bagh massacre (1919).
  7. Lord Irwin (1926–1931): Simon Commission visit (1928), Dandi March (1930), Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931).
  8. Lord Linlithgow (1936–1943): August Offer (1940), Cripps Mission (1942), Quit India Movement (1942).
  9. Lord Mountbatten (1947): June 3 Plan / Mountbatten Plan (partition of India & Pakistan). Last Viceroy & 1st Governor-General of independent India.

  • Immediate Cause: Introduction of Enfield Rifle with grease cartridges made of beef/pig fat. First rebellion by Mangal Pandey (34th Native Infantry) at Barrackpore (29 March 1857).
  • Outbreak: 10 May 1857 at Meerut. Rebels marched to Delhi and declared Bahadur Shah II (Zafar) as Shahenshah-e-Hindustan.

6. Socio-Religious Reform Movements

  • Brahmo Samaj (1828): Founded by Raja Ram Mohan Roy (Father of Indian Renaissance). Opposed Sati, idol worship, caste system. Published Sambad Kaumudi & Mirat-ul-Akbar. Later split into Brahmo Samaj of India (Keshab Chandra Sen) and Sadharan Brahmo Samaj (Ananda Mohan Bose).
  • Arya Samaj (1875): Founded by Swami Dayananda Saraswati at Bombay. Motto: “Go Back to Vedas”. Authored Satyarth Prakash. Rejected idol worship, untouchability. Initiated Shuddhi Movement.
  • Ramakrishna Mission (1897): Founded by Swami Vivekananda at Belur Math. Speech at World Parliament of Religions in Chicago (1893). Periodicals: Prabuddha Bharata & Udbodhana.
  • Young Bengal Movement (1820s-30s): Founded by Henry Vivian Derozio (teacher at Hindu College). Radical intellectual movement.
  • Prarthana Samaj (1867): Founded by Atmaram Pandurang at Bombay (promoted by M.G. Ranade).
  • Satyashodhak Samaj (1873): Founded by Jyotirao Phule in Maharashtra. Fought against caste oppression. Authored Gulamgiri.
  • Self-Respect Movement (1925): Founded by E.V. Ramasamy Naicker (Periyar) in Tamil Nadu.
  • Aligarh Movement: Founded by Sir Syed Ahmad Khan. Established Mohammedan Anglo-Oriental College (1875) at Aligarh (later AMU). Published Tahdhib-ul-Akhlaq.
  • Theosophical Society: Founded by Madame Blavatsky & Col. Olcott (1875, NYC); headquarters shifted to Adyar (Madras). Annie Besant became president in 1907.

7. Indian National Congress (INC) & Freedom Struggle Phases

Formation of INC (1885)

  • Founded by retired civil servant Allan Octavian Hume (A.O. Hume).
  • First Session: December 1885 at Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay. Presided by Womesh Chandra Bonnerjee (W.C. Bonnerjee); attended by 72 delegates. Viceroy was Lord Dufferin.

Three Phases of Freedom Movement

  1. Moderate Phase (1885–1905):
    • Leaders: W.C. Bonnerjee, Dadabhai Naoroji, Pherozeshah Mehta, Gopal Krishna Gokhale, Dinshaw Wacha.
    • Method: 3 Ps - Prayers, Petitions, Protests. Constitutional means.
    • Dadabhai Naoroji: “Grand Old Man of India”. Formulated Drain of Wealth Theory in his book Poverty and Un-British Rule in India. First Indian elected to British House of Commons.
  2. Extremist Phase (1905–1919):
    • Leaders: Lal-Bal-Pal (Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal) & Aurobindo Ghosh.
    • Method: Swadeshi, Boycott of foreign goods, Passive resistance, National education.
    • Bal Gangadhar Tilak: “Lokmanya”. Declared: “Swaraj is my birthright and I shall have it”. Started newspapers Kesari (Marathi) & Mahratta (English). Organised Shivaji & Ganpati festivals.
  3. Gandhian Era (1919–1947): Mass movement based on Ahimsa (Non-violence) and Satyagraha.

8. Key Events & Timeline of National Movement (1905–1947)

  • 1905: Partition of Bengal: Announced by Curzon. Triggered Swadeshi Movement. Amar Sonar Bangla composed by Rabindranath Tagore.
  • 1906: Formation of Muslim League: Founded at Dacca by Aga Khan & Nawab Salimullah.
  • 1907: Surat Split: INC split into Moderates and Extremists at Surat session (Presided by Rash Behari Ghosh).
  • 1909: Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act 1909): Introduced Separate Electorates for Muslims (Lord Minto called Father of Communal Electorate).
  • 1911: Annulment of Partition & Capital Shift: Announced at Delhi Durbar by King George V. Capital shifted from Calcutta to Delhi.
  • 1913: Ghadar Party: Founded at San Francisco by Lala Hardayal & Sohan Singh Bhakna. Published Ghadar journal. Komagata Maru Incident (1914).
  • 1915: Return of Mahatma Gandhi: Gandhi returned to India from South Africa on 9 January 1915 (Pravasi Bharatiya Divas).
  • 1916: Lucknow Pact & Session: Presided by Ambica Charan Mazumdar. Moderates & Extremists reunited; INC and Muslim League signed joint pact.
  • 1916: Home Rule League Movement: Launched independently by B.G. Tilak (Poona) and Annie Besant (Adyar/Madras).
  • 1917–1918: Early Gandhian Movements:
    1. Champaran Satyagraha (1917): First Civil Disobedience; against Tinkathia system (forced Indigo cultivation). Invited by Rajkumar Shukla.
    2. Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918): First Hunger Strike; dispute over Cotton Mill plague bonus.
    3. Kheda Satyagraha (1918): First Non-Cooperation; revenue suspension request due to crop failure.
  • 1919: Rowlatt Act & Jallianwala Bagh Massacre:
    • Rowlatt Act (Black Act - arrest without trial).
    • Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919): General Dyer opened fire on peaceful crowd celebrating Baisakhi at Amritsar. Rabindranath Tagore renounced Knighthood.
  • 1919: Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms (GOI Act 1919): Introduced Dyarchy in Provinces (Transferred & Reserved subjects).
  • 1919–1922: Khilafat & Non-Cooperation Movement (NCM):
    • Khilafat Movement led by Ali Brothers (Maulana Mohammad Ali & Shaukat Ali).
    • NCM launched by Gandhi in 1920 (approved at Nagpur session). Boycott of titles, schools, courts, foreign cloth.
    • Chauri Chaura Incident (5 Feb 1922): Mob burned police station in Gorakhpur, killing 22 policemen. Gandhi withdrawn NCM on 12 Feb 1922.
  • 1923: Swaraj Party: Founded by C.R. Das (President) & Motilal Nehru (Secretary) to contest council elections within Sultanate/British framework.
  • 1925: Kakori Train Action: HRSA revolutionaries (Ram Prasad Bismil, Ashfaqullah Khan, Roshan Singh, Rajendra Lahiri) looted railway treasury.
  • 1927–1928: Simon Commission & Nehru Report:
    • All-white Simon Commission arrived (1928) - “Simon Go Back”. Lala Lajpat Rai died of lathi blow during protest.
    • Nehru Report (1928): Chaired by Motilal Nehru; demanded Dominion Status & fundamental rights. Countered by Jinnah’s 14 Points.
  • 1928: Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA): Founded at Feroz Shah Kotla (Delhi) by Chandrashekhar Azad, Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev.
    • Bhagat Singh & Rajguru assassinated Saunders (1928).
    • Bhagat Singh & Batukeshwar Dutt threw bombs in Central Legislative Assembly (8 April 1929). Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, Rajguru executed on 23 March 1931.
  • 1929: Lahore INC Session (Purna Swaraj): Presided by Jawaharlal Nehru. Resolution passed for Purna Swaraj (Complete Independence). Tri-colour flag unfurled on Ravi river (31 Dec 1929). 26 January 1930 declared First Independence Day.
  • 1930–1931: Civil Disobedience Movement (CDM) & Dandi March:
    • Dandi March (12 March – 6 April 1930): Gandhi walked 240 miles from Sabarmati Ashram to Dandi with 78 followers to break Salt Law.
    • Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931): Signed to end CDM; Gandhi agreed to attend 2nd Round Table Conference.
  • 1930–1932: Round Table Conferences (RTC):
    • Held in London. B.R. Ambedkar & Tej Bahadur Sapru attended all three RTCs. Gandhi attended ONLY 2nd RTC (1931).
  • 1932: Communal Award & Poona Pact:
    • Ramsay MacDonald announced Communal Award giving separate electorates to Depressed Classes.
    • Poona Pact (24 Sept 1932): Signed between B.R. Ambedkar & Gandhi (in Yerwada Jail); abandoned separate electorates in favor of reserved seats for Depressed Classes.
  • 1935: Government of India Act 1935:
    • Abolished Dyarchy in Provinces; introduced Provincial Autonomy.
    • Proposed All-India Federation; introduced Dyarchy at the Centre.
    • Established Federal Court & Reserve Bank of India (RBI).
  • 1939: Resignation of Congress Ministries: Congress ministries resigned protesting India’s inclusion in WWII without consent. Muslim League celebrated Deliverance Day (22 Dec 1939).
  • 1940: Lahore Resolution (Pakistan Resolution): Muslim League demanded separate nation for Muslims.
  • 1940: August Offer: Viceroy Linlithgow proposed expanded Executive Council & post-war constitution drafting body. Rejected by Congress.
  • 1940: Individual Satyagraha: Launched by Gandhi. 1st Satyagrahi: Vinoba Bhave, 2nd: Jawaharlal Nehru.
  • 1942: Cripps Mission: Sir Stafford Cripps offered post-war Dominion Status. Gandhi called it a “Post-dated cheque on a failing bank”.
  • 1942: Quit India Movement (August Kranti): Passed at Gowalia Tank (Bombay) on 8 August 1942. Gandhi gave slogan “Do or Die”. All top leaders arrested under Operation Zero Hour. Aruna Asaf Ali hoisted flag. Parallel governments formed at Ballia (Chittu Pandey), Tamluk, Satara.
  • 1943–1945: Indian National Army (INA) & Subhas Chandra Bose:
    • INA originally conceived by Mohan Singh; reorganized by Subhas Chandra Bose (“Netaji”) in Singapore (1943). Slogans: “Jai Hind”, “Give me blood, I will give you freedom”. Formed Azad Hind Government.
  • 1945: Shimla Conference & Wavell Plan: Failed due to Muslim League’s insistence on being sole Muslim representative.
  • 1946: Royal Indian Navy (RIN) Mutiny: Mutiny on HMIS Talwar at Bombay (Feb 1946).
  • 1946: Cabinet Mission Plan: Members: Stafford Cripps, Pethick-Lawrence, A.V. Alexander. Rejected Pakistan; proposed 3-tier grouping. Led to formation of Interim Government (Sept 1946) headed by Nehru and Constituent Assembly.
  • 1946: Direct Action Day (16 August 1946): Called by Jinnah / Muslim League; resulted in widespread communal riots (Great Calcutta Killings).
  • 1947: Mountbatten Plan & Indian Independence Act:
    • June 3 Plan: Mountbatten announced partition of British India.
    • Indian Independence Act 1947: Passed by British Parliament; India gained independence on 15 August 1947.

9. Important National Leaders & Contributions

  • Raja Ram Mohan Roy: Published Tuhfat-ul-Muwahhidin, Sambad Kaumudi. Founded Brahmo Samaj.
  • Dadabhai Naoroji: Wrote Poverty and Un-British Rule in India. 3-time INC President.
  • Bal Gangadhar Tilak: Newspapers Kesari & Mahratta. Books: Gita Rahasya, The Arctic Home in the Vedas.
  • Gopal Krishna Gokhale: Political guru of Gandhi. Founded Servants of India Society (1905).
  • Mahatma Gandhi: Authored Hind Swaraj, autobiography The Story of My Experiments with Truth. Edited Young India, Navjivan, Harijan.
  • Subhas Chandra Bose: Passed ICS exam; resigned. INC President in 1938 (Haripura) & 1939 (Tripuri). Resigned and formed Forward Bloc (1939).
  • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar: Chaired Drafting Committee of Constitution. Founded Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha (1924), Independent Labour Party. Led Mahad Satyagraha (1927).
  • Sarojini Naidu: “Nightingale of India”. First Indian woman INC President (Kanpur session, 1925). First woman Governor of an Indian state (UP).
  • Annie Besant: Founded Home Rule League. First woman INC President (Calcutta session, 1917). Edited New India & Commonweal.

Part 4: World History

1. Major Revolutions & Key Events

American War of Independence (1775–1783)

  • Causes: Taxations without representation (Stamp Act 1765, Townshend Acts). Slogan: “No Taxation without Representation”.
  • Boston Tea Party (16 Dec 1773): Samuel Adams & Sons of Liberty dumped British tea into Boston harbor.
  • Declaration of Independence (4 July 1776): Drafted by Thomas Jefferson at Philadelphia Continental Congress.
  • Key Figures: George Washington (Commander of Continental Army, 1st US President).
  • Treaty of Paris (1783): Britain recognized American independence.

French Revolution (1789–1799)

  • Causes: Autocratic rule of Louis XVI (Bourbon dynasty) and Marie Antoinette. Division of society into Three Estates (1st: Clergy, 2nd: Nobility, 3rd: Commoners/Peasants). Heavy tax Burden on 3rd Estate (Taille, Tithe).
  • Key Events:
    • Storming of the Bastille (14 July 1789): Symbolized fall of absolute monarchy.
    • Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen: Idealized Liberty, Equality, Fraternity.
    • Reign of Terror (1793–94): Led by Maximilien Robespierre (Jacobin Club); used Guillotine.
  • Rise of Napoleon Bonaparte: Seized power in 1799 (Coup of 18 Brumaire); crowned Emperor (1804). Defeated at Battle of Waterloo (1815) by Duke of Wellington.

Russian Revolution (1917)

  • Causes: Tyrannical rule of Tsar Nicholas II (Romanov dynasty). Bloody Sunday (1905). Heavy losses in WWI.
  • Feb/March Revolution (1917): Overthrew Tsar; established provisional government under Alexander Kerensky.
  • Oct/Nov Bolshevik Revolution (1917): Led by Vladimir Lenin & Bolshevik Party. Overthrew provisional government. Slogan: “Peace, Land, and Bread”. Established USSR (1922).

2. World War I & II

World War I (1914–1918)

  • Immediate Cause: Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria and his wife in Sarajevo (28 June 1914) by Gavrilo Princip (Black Hand group).
  • Alliances:
    • Allied Powers: UK, France, Russia, Italy, Japan, USA (entered 1917).
    • Central Powers: Germany, Austria-Hungary, Ottoman Empire, Bulgaria.
  • Outcome: Central Powers defeated. Treaty of Versailles (1919) imposed harsh reparations on Germany. League of Nations formed (1920).

World War II (1939–1945)

  • Immediate Cause: German invasion of Poland (1 Sept 1939) under Adolf Hitler (Fascism/Nazism).
  • Axis Powers: Germany, Italy (Mussolini), Japan.
  • Allied Powers: UK (Winston Churchill), USSR (Joseph Stalin), USA (F.D. Roosevelt/Truman), France.
  • Key Turning Points:
    • Battle of Stalingrad (1942–43): Soviet victory over Germany.
    • Pearl Harbor Attack (7 Dec 1941): Japan attacked US naval base; brought USA into war.
    • D-Day / Normandy Landings (6 June 1944).
  • End of War: Atomic bombings of Hiroshima (6 Aug 1945 - Little Boy) & Nagasaki (9 Aug 1945 - Fat Man) by USA. Japan surrendered 15 Aug 1945. United Nations (UN) established on 24 October 1945.

Part 5: Art, Architecture & Culture Quick-Reference

1. Major Architectural Styles & Temples

  • Nagara Style (North India): Square plan with curvilinear tower (Shikhara). Examples: Sun Temple (Konark), Khajuraho Temples (Chandelas), Dilwara Jain Temples (Mount Abu).
  • Dravida Style (South India): Pyramid tower (Vimana), large gateway (Gopuram). Examples: Brihadeshwara Temple (Tanjore - Cholas), Shore Temple (Mamallapuram - Pallavas).
  • Vesara Style (Central/Deccan): Hybrid of Nagara and Dravida. Examples: Hoysaleswara Temple (Halebid - Hoysalas), Badami Cave Temples (Chalukyas).

2. Classical Dances of India (8 Forms)

  1. Bharatanatyam: Tamil Nadu
  2. Kathakali: Kerala (Expressive face mask & makeup)
  3. Kathak: North India (UP)
  4. Odissi: Odisha
  5. Manipuri: Manipur
  6. Kuchipudi: Andhra Pradesh
  7. Mohiniyattam: Kerala
  8. Sattriya: Assam (Introduced by Srimanta Sankardev)
