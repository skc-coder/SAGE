Plate Tectonics, Mountains and Volcanism

Comprehensive, exhaustive master concept notes on Plate Tectonics Theory, Continental Drift, Mountain Building (Orogeny), Faulting, Earthquake Dynamics, and Volcanic Landforms synthesized from Pages 78-95 of the Physical Geography PDF.

Theory, Intuition & Key Concepts

1. Continental Drift & Plate Tectonics Theory

A. Continental Drift Theory (Alfred Wegener, 1912)

  • Proposed that all continents were once merged into a supercontinent called Pangea surrounded by a mega-ocean called Panthalassa.
  • Around 200 million years ago, Pangea split into:
    1. Laurasia (Angaraland): Northern mass (North America, Europe, Asia).
    2. Gondwanaland: Southern mass (South America, Africa, India, Australia, Antarctica).
    • Separated by the Tethys Sea.
  • Forces Proposed by Wegener: Pole-fleeing force (centrifugal force due to rotation) and Tidal force (sun/moon attraction).

B. Evidences for Continental Drift

  1. Jig-Saw Fit: Coasts of South America and Africa fit together perfectly.
  2. Rocks of Same Age across Oceans: 2,000 million-year-old rock belts of Brazil match West Africa.
  3. Tillite Deposits: Glacial sedimentary rocks found in India, Africa, Madagascar, Australia, and Antarctica.
  4. Placer Deposits: Rich gold deposits in Ghana coast without source rock (source rock is in Brazil).
  5. Fossil Evidence: Mesosaurus (freshwater reptile) fossils found only in Brazil and South Africa.

C. Plate Tectonics Theory (McKenzie, Parker & Morgan, 1967)

  • Lithosphere is broken into 7 Major Plates and several Minor Plates floating on the asthenosphere.
  • 7 Major Plates:
    1. Pacific Plate (Largest, oceanic)
    2. North American Plate
    3. South American Plate
    4. Eurasian Plate
    5. African Plate
    6. Indo-Australian-New Zealand Plate
    7. Antarctic Plate
  • Important Minor Plates:
    • Cocos Plate: Between Central America and Pacific Plate.
    • Nazca Plate: Between South America and Pacific Plate.
    • Arabian Plate: Saudi Arabian landmass.
    • Philippine Plate: Between Asiatic and Pacific plate.
    • Caroline Plate: Between Philippine and Indian plate.
    • Fuji Plate: North-east of Australia.

2. Types of Plate Boundaries & Landforms

  1. Divergent (Constructive) Boundary:

    • Plates move away from each other.
    • Magma rises from asthenosphere, creating new oceanic crust.
    • Landforms: Mid-Oceanic Ridges (e.g., Mid-Atlantic Ridge) and Continental Rift Valleys (e.g., East African Rift Valley).

  2. Convergent (Destructive) Boundary:

    • Plates move towards each other. Denser oceanic plate subducts under lighter plate into the mantle (Subduction Zone / Benioff Zone).
    • Types of Convergence:
      • Oceanic-Continental: Volcanic arcs, Deep Ocean Trenches (e.g., Andes Mountains, Peru-Chile Trench).
      • Oceanic-Oceanic: Island Arcs / Archipelagoes, Deep Ocean Trenches (e.g., Mariana Trench - 11,034 m, Japan Arc).
      • Continental-Continental: Fold Mountains (e.g., Himalayas, Alps). Zero subduction/volcanoes!

  3. Transform (Conservative) Boundary:

    • Plates slide horizontally past each other. No crust created or destroyed.
    • Landforms: Transform faults (e.g., San Andreas Fault, California). High earthquake frequency!

3. Mountains Classification & Volcanoes

A. Classification of Mountains

  1. Fold Mountains: Formed by compressive tectonic forces (e.g., Young Fold: Himalayas, Alps, Andes, Rockies; Old Fold: Aravallis, Appalachians, Urals).
  2. Block Mountains (Fault-Block): Formed by tensional forces causing faulting. Uplifted block = Horst, Downthrown block = Graben / Rift Valley (e.g., Black Forest & Vosges in Europe, Vindhyas & Satpuras in India).
  3. Volcanic Mountains: Formed by accumulation of volcanic material (e.g., Mt. Fuji - Japan, Mt. Kilimanjaro - Tanzania, Mt. Cotopaxi - Ecuador).
  4. Residual / Dissected Mountains: Denuded by erosion (e.g., Aravallis, Rajmahal hills, Eastern Ghats).

B. Types of Magma & Volcanic Features

  • Basaltic Magma: Fluid, low silica (≈50%), high temperature, non-explosive, forms Shield Volcanoes (e.g., Hawaiian volcanoes) and Lava Plateaus (Deccan Traps).
  • Granitic / Andesitic Magma: Viscous, high silica (≈70%), explosive, forms Composite / Stratovolcanoes (e.g., Mt. Vesuvius, Mt. St. Helens).
