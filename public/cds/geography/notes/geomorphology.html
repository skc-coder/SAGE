Geomorphology and Earth Interior

Comprehensive, exhaustive master concept notes on the interior structure of the Earth, crustal composition, seismic wave dynamics, discontinuities, and direct/indirect sources of geological data synthesized from Pages 20-35 of the Physical Geography PDF.

Theory, Intuition & Key Concepts

1. Sources of Information about Earth’s Interior

A. Direct Sources

  1. Surface Rock Samples: Mining rocks and surface observations (e.g., deep gold mines in South Africa like Mponeng Gold Mine reaching 3−4 km depth).
  2. Deep Earth Drilling Projects:
    • Deep Ocean Drilling Project: Drilling oceanic crust.
    • Kola Superdeep Borehole (Russia): Deepest artificial point on Earth, reaching 12,262 m (12.2 km). Temperatures reached 180∘C, halting deeper drilling.
  3. Volcanic Eruptions: Magma brought to the surface from depths of ≈100−200 km (Asthenosphere) provides direct chemical samples of the upper mantle.

B. Indirect Sources

  1. Meteors / Meteorites: Extra-terrestrial solid bodies formed from the same primordial nebular material as Earth. Core compositions of iron-nickel meteorites mirror Earth’s core.
  2. Seismic Waves: Analysis of earthquake body wave propagation (P-waves and S-waves) through different rock densities.
  3. Gravity Anomaly: Variations in gravitational acceleration (g) across the globe due to uneven mass distribution (g is higher at the poles than at the equator due to centrifugal force and Earth’s flattened polar radius).
  4. Magnetic Field & Palaeomagnetism: Earth’s geomagnetic field generated by liquid outer core dynamo action.
  5. Temperature & Pressure Gradients: Temperature and pressure increase systematically with depth (≈1∘C per 32 meters in the upper crust).

2. Earth’s Interior Layering & Chemical Composition

A. Chemical Classification (Suess Model: SIAL, SIMA, NIFE)

  • SIAL (Crust): Rich in Silica (Si) & Aluminium (Al). Light granitic rocks. Density ≈2.7 g/cm3.
  • SIMA (Mantle): Rich in Silica (Si) & Magnesium (Mg). Dense basaltic rocks. Density ≈3.5−5.5 g/cm3.
  • NIFE (Core): Rich in Nickel (Ni) & Iron (Fe). High density metallic core. Density ≈11−13 g/cm3.

B. Mechanical Layering (Crust, Mantle, Core)

1. The Crust
  • Outermost thin solid shell.
  • Oceanic Crust: Thin (≈5−10 km thick), dense (≈3.0 g/cm3), composed primarily of Basalt (rich in silica, iron, and magnesium).
  • Continental Crust: Thick (≈35−70 km thick, up to 70−100 km beneath major mountain ranges like the Himalayas), less dense (≈2.7 g/cm3), composed primarily of Granite.
2. The Mantle
  • Extends from the base of the crust down to a depth of 2,900 km. Makes up ≈84% of Earth’s volume and 67% of Earth’s mass.
  • Asthenosphere:
    • Upper portion of the mantle extending from ≈80−200 km depth.
    • Highly ductile, semi-fluid / plastic state (“weak zone” — Astheno means weak).
    • Main source of magma during volcanic eruptions.
    • Tectonic plates of the Lithosphere float atop the ductile Asthenosphere.
  • Lithosphere: Rigid outer layer comprising the Crust + Uppermost Solid Mantle (≈10−200 km thick).
3. The Core
  • Extends from 2,900 km down to the center of Earth (6,371 km).
  • Outer Core: Liquid state (prohibited from transmitting S-waves). Generates Earth’s magnetic field via geodynamo action.
  • Inner Core: Solid state due to immense overburden pressure, despite extreme temperature (≈5500−6000∘C).

3. Seismic Waves & Discontinuities

A. Seismic Waves Breakdown

  1. Body Waves: Travel through Earth’s interior.
    • Primary (P) Waves: Longitudinal / compressional waves (particles vibrate parallel to wave direction). Fastest waves (v≈6−13 km/s). Travel through solids, liquids, and gases.
    • Secondary (S) Waves: Transverse / shear waves (particles vibrate perpendicular to wave direction). Slower (v≈3.5−7 km/s). Travel ONLY through solids (blocked by liquid outer core).
  2. Surface Waves (L-waves & Rayleigh waves): Travel along Earth’s surface. Slowest, highest amplitude, cause maximum destruction.

B. Internal Discontinuities

Boundary zones where seismic wave velocity changes abruptly due to density shifts:

  1. Conrad Discontinuity: Between Upper Granitic Crust and Lower Basaltic Crust.
  2. Mohorovičić (Moho) Discontinuity: Between Crust and Upper Mantle.
  3. Repetti Discontinuity: Between Upper Mantle and Lower Mantle.
  4. Gutenberg Discontinuity: Between Lower Mantle and Liquid Outer Core (2,900 km).
  5. Lehmann Discontinuity: Between Liquid Outer Core and Solid Inner Core (5,150 km).

4. Elemental Abundance Breakdown

A. Abundance of Elements in Entire Earth (By Mass)

Iron (Fe≈32.1%)>Oxygen (O≈30.1%)>Silicon (Si≈15.1%)>Magnesium (Mg≈13.9%)>Sulfur (S≈2.9%)>Nickel (Ni≈1.8%)

B. Abundance of Elements in Earth’s Crust (By Mass)

Oxygen (O≈46.6%)>Silicon (Si≈27.7%)>Aluminium (Al≈8.1%)>Iron (Fe≈5.0%)>Calcium (Ca≈3.6%)>Sodium (Na≈2.8%)

Exam Distinction / Trap:

  • Most abundant element in Entire Earth: Iron (Fe).
  • Most abundant element in Earth’s Crust: Oxygen (O).
  • Most abundant Metal in Earth’s Crust: Aluminium (Al).

Detailed PYQ Analysis (Pages 20-30)

PYQ 1. Lithosphere Definition (CDS II 2025 / CAPF)

  • Question: Which one of the following best describes the Lithosphere?
    • (a) Upper and lower mantle
    • (b) Crust and core
    • (c) Crust and uppermost solid mantle [Correct]
    • (d) Lower mantle and core

PYQ 2. Earth Crust Element Ascending Order (CDS 2024)

  • Question: Arrange the following major elements present in the Earth’s crust in ascending order by weight percentage: 1. Aluminium, 2. Calcium, 3. Silicon, 4. Oxygen.
    • Ascending Order: Calcium (3.6%) → Aluminium (8.1%) → Silicon (27.7%) → Oxygen (46.6%).
