Pressure Belts, Local Winds and Cyclones

Comprehensive, exhaustive master concept notes on Global Pressure Belts, Planetary Winds, Local Winds of the World, Jet Streams, and Tropical vs Temperate Cyclones synthesized from Pages 163-184 of the Physical Geography PDF.

Theory, Intuition & Key Concepts

1. Global Pressure Belts & Planetary Winds

A. The 7 World Pressure Belts

  1. Equatorial Low Pressure Belt (5∘ N to 5∘ S): Thermal origin. Intense solar heating causes air to expand and rise. Region of calm winds called Doldrums.
  2. Subtropical High Pressure Belts (30∘−35∘ N & S): Dynamic origin. Sinking dry air from upper atmosphere. Region of calm, dry weather called Horse Latitudes.
  3. Subpolar Low Pressure Belts (60∘−65∘ N & S): Dynamic origin. Convergence of warm westerlies and cold polar easterlies.
  4. Polar High Pressure Belts (90∘ N & S): Thermal origin. Intense cold causes air to contract and sink.

B. Planetary / Primary Winds & Ferrel’s Law

  • Coriolis Force: Deflective force caused by Earth’s rotation.
    • Deflects winds to the RIGHT in the Northern Hemisphere and to the LEFT in the Southern Hemisphere (Ferrel’s Law / Buys Ballot’s Law).
    • Coriolis force is 0 at Equator and maximum at Poles.
  1. Trade Winds: Flow from Subtropical High (30∘) to Equatorial Low (0∘). NE Trades in North, SE Trades in South.
  2. Westerlies: Flow from Subtropical High (30∘) to Subpolar Low (60∘). Strongest in Southern Hemisphere oceans (“Roaring Forties”, “Furious Fifties”, “Shrieking Sixties”).
  3. Polar Easterlies: Flow from Polar High (90∘) to Subpolar Low (60∘).

2. Local Winds of the World

A. Warm Local Winds

  • Chinook: Warm dry wind blowing down eastern slopes of Rocky Mountains (USA/Canada). Known as the “Snow Eater”.
  • Foehn: Warm dry wind blowing down northern slopes of the Alps (Europe).
  • Sirocco: Hot, dry, dust-laden wind blowing from Sahara Desert across Mediterranean Sea towards Southern Europe. Causes “Blood Rain” in Italy due to red desert dust!
  • Harmattan: Dry, dusty wind blowing from Sahara towards West Africa. Known as the “Doctor” because it provides relief from humid coastal air.
  • Loo: Hot, dry, scorching summer wind in Northern Plains of India & Pakistan.
  • Santa Ana: Hot, dry wind in Southern California causing wildfires.
  • Zonda: Warm dry wind in Argentina (Andes).

B. Cold Local Winds

  • Mistral: Cold dry wind blowing from Alps down Rhone Valley to Mediterranean (France).
  • Bora: Cold, dry, gusty wind blowing from Adriatic coast.
  • Blizzard: Cold snow-bearing wind in Canada, Russia, and USA.
  • Pampero: Cold wind in Pampas of Argentina.
  • Buran: Cold wind in Siberia/Central Asia.

3. Cyclones: Tropical vs Temperate (Frontal)

A. Conditions for Tropical Cyclone Formation

  1. Large ocean surface with temperature >27∘C.
  2. Presence of Coriolis Force (cannot form at Equator 0∘−5∘!).
  3. Small variations in vertical wind speed (low wind shear).
  4. Pre-existing weak low-pressure area.

B. Comparison Table: Tropical vs Temperate Cyclones

PropertyTropical CyclonesTemperate (Extra-Tropical) Cyclones
OriginThermal origin (latent heat of condensation over warm seas).Dynamic origin (Frontal collision of warm & cold air masses).
Location8∘−20∘ N & S (Tropical oceans only).35∘−65∘ N & S (Over land and sea).
Frontal SystemNo Fronts present.Clear Frontal Systems (Warm Front & Cold Front).
Eye of StormWell-defined calm Eye at center.No distinct central eye.
MovementMove East to West (driven by Trade winds).Move West to East (driven by Westerlies).
DestructivenessExtremely violent, high wind speeds (>200 km/h), short life.Less violent, cover vast areas, last for days.

C. Regional Names of Tropical Cyclones

  • Hurricanes: Atlantic Ocean & Eastern Pacific (Caribbean, USA).
  • Typhoons: Western Pacific & China Sea (Japan, China, Philippines).
  • Cyclones: Indian Ocean (Bay of Bengal & Arabian Sea).
  • Willy-Willies: Western Australia.
  • Tornadoes: Mississippi Valley, USA (most violent small-scale vortex, measured on Fujita Scale).
