Indian Polity Cheat Sheet

Overview & Core Concepts

The Constitution of India is the supreme law of the land, adopted on 26th November 1949 (enacted/came into full force on 26th January 1950). Originally containing 395 Articles, 8 Schedules, and 22 Parts, it has evolved through constitutional amendments into the lengthiest written constitution of any sovereign country in the world.

1. Key Historical Acts & Constitutional Evolution

Historical Act / MilestoneYearKey Provisions & Impact
Regulating Act1773First constitutional intervention by British Govt. Designated Governor of Bengal as Governor-General of Bengal (Warren Hastings). Established Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774) with Sir Elijah Impey as Chief Justice.
Pitt’s India Act1784Separated commercial & political functions of EIC. Created Board of Control (6 Commissioners) to manage political affairs while Court of Directors managed commercial affairs (Dual system of control).
Charter Act1793Extended EIC trade monopoly for 20 years. Salaries of Board of Control paid out of Indian revenues.
Charter Act1813Abolished EIC trade monopoly (except trade in Tea and trade with China). Allocated ₹1 Lakh annually for Indian education. Allowed Christian missionaries into India.
Charter Act1833Governor-General of Bengal renamed Governor-General of India (Lord William Bentinck - 1st GGI). Complete end of EIC trading functions (became purely administrative body). Law Member added to Governor-General’s Council (Lord Macaulay).
Charter Act1853Separated executive & legislative functions of Governor-General’s Council. Introduced open competitive examination for Civil Services (Macaulay Committee, 1854).
Government of India Act1858Transferred power from EIC to British Crown (Good Governance Act). GGI designated as Viceroy of India (Lord Canning - 1st Viceroy). Created office of Secretary of State for India assisted by a 15-member Council.
Indian Councils Act1861Initiated portfolio system (introduced by Canning in 1859). Restored legislative powers to Bombay & Madras (Decentralisation). Empowered Viceroy to issue Ordinances (life 6 months).
Indian Councils Act1892Increased non-official members in councils. Allowed discussion on Annual Budget & asking questions to executive.
Morley-Minto Reforms1909Introduced Communal Electorate for Muslims (Lord Minto = Father of Communal Electorate). First Indian in Viceroy’s Executive Council (Satyendra Prasad Sinha).
Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms1919Introduced Dyarchy in Provinces (Transferred vs Reserved subjects). Established Bicameralism & Direct Elections at Centre. Created Central Public Service Commission (established 1926).
Simon Commission19277-member statutory commission (all British) chaired by Sir John Simon to review 1919 Act. Recommended abolition of Dyarchy and setup of federation.
Government of India Act1935Detailed act (321 Sections, 10 Schedules). Proposed All-India Federation. Divided powers into 3 lists: Federal (59), Provincial (54), Concurrent (36); Residuary powers with Viceroy. Abolished Provincial Dyarchy & introduced Provincial Autonomy. Introduced Dyarchy at Centre (never operationalised). Established Federal Court (1937) & RBI (1935). Bicameralism in 6 out of 11 provinces.
August Offer1940Proposed expansion of Viceroy’s Executive Council & Constituent Assembly post-WW2.
Cripps Mission1942Sir Stafford Cripps proposed dominion status post-WW2 (Rejected by Congress & Muslim League; Gandhi called it a “Post-dated cheque”).
Cabinet Mission Plan1946Members: Pethick-Lawrence, Stafford Cripps, A.V. Alexander. Rejected two-nation state idea; recommended setup of Constituent Assembly to draft Indian Constitution.
Indian Independence Act1947Enacted Mountbatten Plan (3rd June 1947). Ended British Rule; created two independent Dominions: India & Pakistan (w.e.f. 15th August 1947). Abolished Secretary of State post.

2. Making of the Constitution & Key Committees

Important Dates & Events

  • First Idea of Constituent Assembly: M.N. Roy (1934); officially demanded by INC in 1935.
  • First Meeting of Constituent Assembly: 9th December 1946 (Temporary President: Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha).
  • Permanent President Elected: 11th December 1946 (Dr. Rajendra Prasad; Vice-Presidents: H.C. Mookherjee & V.T. Krishnamachari; Constitutional Advisor: Sir B.N. Rau).
  • Objective Resolution: Introduced by Jawaharlal Nehru on 13th December 1946 (Unanimously adopted on 22nd January 1947; formed basis of Preamble).
  • Drafting Committee Formed: 29th August 1947 (7 members; Chairman: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar).
  • Adoption of National Flag: 22nd July 1947.
  • Adoption of Constitution: 26th November 1949 (Constitution Day / Samvidhan Divas).
  • Adoption of National Anthem & Song: 24th January 1950 (Final session of Assembly).
  • Constitution Came Into Effect: 26th January 1950 (Republic Day).
  • Time Taken: 2 Years, 11 Months, and 18 Days (Total 11 sessions, ₹64 Lakh expense).

Key Drafting Committee Members (7 Members)

  1. Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (Chairman - Father of the Indian Constitution)
  2. N. Gopalaswamy Ayyangar
  3. Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar
  4. Dr. K.M. Munshi
  5. Syed Mohammad Saadullah
  6. N. Madhava Rau (replaced B.L. Mitter due to ill-health)
  7. T.T. Krishnamachari (replaced D.P. Khaitan who died in 1948)

4. Parts, Schedules & Preamble Breakdown

The Preamble

  • Keywords: “SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC”
  • Ideals: Justice (Social, Economic, Political); Liberty (Thought, Expression, Belief, Faith, Worship); Equality (Status and Opportunity); Fraternity (Assuring dignity & unity).
  • Amendability of Preamble:
    • Berubari Union Case (1960): SC held Preamble is NOT part of Constitution.
    • Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973): SC reversed earlier decision; held Preamble IS part of Constitution and can be amended under Art. 368 without altering the Basic Structure.
    • 42nd Amendment (1976): Amended Preamble only ONCE so far—added 3 words: Socialist, Secular, and Integrity.

Important Schedules (Original: 8, Present: 12)

  • 1st Schedule: Names of States & Union Territories.
  • 2nd Schedule: Emoluments, allowances & privileges of President, Governors, Speaker, Judges, CAG.
  • 3rd Schedule: Forms of Oaths & Affirmations.
  • 4th Schedule: Allocation of seats in Rajya Sabha to States & UTs.
  • 5th Schedule: Administration & control of Scheduled Areas & Scheduled Tribes.
  • 6th Schedule: Administration of Tribal areas in 4 North-Eastern States (AMTM: Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram).
  • 7th Schedule: 3 Legislative Lists (Union, State, Concurrent).
  • 8th Schedule: 22 Recognized Languages (Original: 14; 21st Amend added Sindhi; 71st added Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali; 92nd added Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali).
  • 9th Schedule: Added by 1st Amendment (1951); validation of land reform acts (protected from judicial review, subject to I.R. Coelho ruling post-1973).
  • 10th Schedule: Added by 52nd Amendment (1985); Anti-Defection Law.
  • 11th Schedule: Added by 73rd Amendment (1992); Panchayati Raj (29 Functional Matters).
  • 12th Schedule: Added by 74th Amendment (1992); Municipalities (18 Functional Matters).

5. Important Articles & Fundamental Rights Checklist

Parts of the Constitution Summary

  • Part I: Union & Its Territory (Art. 1–4)
  • Part II: Citizenship (Art. 5–11)
  • Part III: Fundamental Rights (Art. 12–35)
  • Part IV: Directive Principles of State Policy (Art. 36–51)
  • Part IVA: Fundamental Duties (Art. 51A)
  • Part V: The Union Executive & Parliament (Art. 52–151)
  • Part VI: The State Executive & Legislature (Art. 152–237)
  • Part IX / IXA / IXB: Panchayats / Municipalities / Co-operatives (Art. 243–243ZT)
  • Part XI: Centre-State Relations (Art. 245–263)
  • Part XV: Elections (Art. 324–329A)
  • Part XVIII: Emergency Provisions (Art. 352–360)
  • Part XX: Amendment of Constitution (Art. 368)

Fundamental Rights (Part III, Art. 12–35) — Magna Carta of India

  1. Right to Equality (Art. 14–18):
    • Art 14: Equality before law & Equal protection of laws.
    • Art 15: Prohibition of discrimination (religion, race, caste, sex, place of birth).
    • Art 16: Equality of opportunity in public employment.
    • Art 17: Abolition of Untouchability.
    • Art 18: Abolition of Titles.
  2. Right to Freedom (Art. 19–22):
    • Art 19: 6 Freedoms (Speech, Assembly, Association, Movement, Residence, Profession).
    • Art 20: Protection in respect of conviction for offences (No Ex-post facto law, No Double Jeopardy, No Self-incrimination).
    • Art 21: Protection of Life and Personal Liberty (Expanded in Maneka Gandhi Case 1978).
    • Art 21A: Right to Education (6–14 yrs; added by 86th Amendment 2002).
    • Art 22: Protection against arrest and detention in certain cases.
  3. Right against Exploitation (Art. 23–24):
    • Art 23: Prohibition of human trafficking & forced labour (Begar).
    • Art 24: Prohibition of child labour in hazardous industries (under 14 yrs).
  4. Right to Freedom of Religion (Art. 25–28):
    • Art 25: Freedom of conscience, free profession, practice and propagation.
    • Art 26: Freedom to manage religious affairs.
    • Art 27: Freedom from payment of taxes for promotion of any religion.
    • Art 28: Freedom from attending religious instruction in educational institutions.
  5. Cultural & Educational Rights (Art. 29–30):
    • Art 29: Protection of interests of minorities.
    • Art 30: Right of minorities to establish & administer educational institutions.
  6. Right to Constitutional Remedies (Art. 32):
    • Dr. B.R. Ambedkar called Article 32 “The Very Heart and Soul of the Constitution”.
    • 5 Constitutional Writs:
      1. Habeas Corpus (“To have the body of”): Against arbitrary detention (issued against public & private entities).
      2. Mandamus (“We Command”): Compels public official to perform statutory duty (NOT against President/Governor).
      3. Prohibition (“To forbid”): Issued by higher court to lower court/tribunal to prevent exceeding jurisdiction.
      4. Certiorari (“To be certified/informed”): Quashes order of lower court/tribunal (preventive & curative).
      5. Quo-Warranto (“By what authority”): Inquires into legality of claim of a person to a public office.

Note: Fundamental Right to Property (Art 31) was deleted by 44th Amendment (1978) and made a Legal Right under Article 300A (Part XII).

7. Major Constitutional Amendments Cheat Sheet

Amendment ActYearKey Changes & Landmark Impacts
1st Amendment1951Added 9th Schedule to protect land reform laws from judicial review; placed reasonable restrictions on freedom of speech.
7th Amendment1956Reorganised States on linguistic basis (Fazl Ali Commission); abolished Class A, B, C, D states; introduced Union Territories; provided for common High Courts for 2 or more states.
24th Amendment1971Affirmed Parliament’s power to amend any part of Constitution including Fundamental Rights under Art 368. Made President’s assent mandatory for Constitutional Amendment Bills.
36th Amendment1975Made Sikkim a full-fledged 22nd State of the Indian Union.
42nd Amendment1976Known as “Mini-Constitution” (Swaran Singh Committee). Added words Socialist, Secular, Integrity to Preamble. Added Part IVA (Art 51A - 10 Fundamental Duties). Transferred 5 subjects (Education, Forests, Weights & Measures, Protection of Wild Animals, Administration of Justice) from State to Concurrent List.
44th Amendment1978Enacted by Janata Party Govt to undo 42nd Act excesses. Removed Right to Property from Fundamental Rights (made legal right under Art 300A). Replaced “Internal Disturbance” with “Armed Rebellion” for National Emergency (Art 352). Stipulated Articles 20 & 21 cannot be suspended even during Emergency.
52nd Amendment1985Added 10th Schedule (Anti-Defection Law); disqualified MPs/MLAs for defecting from political party.
61st Amendment1989Lowered voting age for Lok Sabha and Assembly elections from 21 to 18 years (amended Art 326).
69th Amendment1991Accorded special status to Union Territory of Delhi; designated it as National Capital Territory (NCT) of Delhi with 70-member Legislative Assembly.
73rd Amendment1992Granted constitutional status to Panchayati Raj Institutions (Added Part IX & 11th Schedule with 29 functional items).
74th Amendment1992Granted constitutional status to Urban Local Bodies / Municipalities (Added Part IXA & 12th Schedule with 18 functional items).
86th Amendment2002Made free and compulsory education a Fundamental Right (Added Article 21A for 6–14 yrs). Added 11th Fundamental Duty under Art 51A(k) for parents/guardians.
91st Amendment2003Restricted total number of ministers (including PM/CM) to 15% of total strength of Lok Sabha / State Assembly (minimum 12 in State Assembly).
92nd Amendment2003Added 4 languages to 8th Schedule: Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santhali (total 22).
100th Amendment2015Land Boundary Agreement (LBA) between India and Bangladesh (exchange of enclaves).
101st Amendment2016Introduced Goods and Services Tax (GST) (enacted 1st July 2017); added Article 279A (GST Council).
102nd Amendment2018Granted Constitutional status to National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC) (Art 338B).
103rd Amendment2019Provided 10% Reservation for Economically Weaker Sections (EWS) in education & public employment (Art 15(6) & 16(6)).
104th Amendment2020Extended SC/ST seat reservation in Lok Sabha and Assemblies for 10 years (till 2030); abolished nomination of Anglo-Indians to Lok Sabha and Assemblies.
105th Amendment2021Restored power of State Governments to identify and specify socially and educationally backward classes (SEBCs).
106th Amendment2023Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam: Reserves 1/3rd (33%) of seats for women in Lok Sabha, State Assemblies, and Delhi Assembly for 15 years.

8. Landmark Supreme Court Cases Quick-Ref

  • Shankari Prasad Case (1951) & Sajjan Singh Case (1965): SC held Parliament can amend any part of Constitution including Fundamental Rights.
  • Golaknath Case (1967): SC ruled Fundamental Rights are sacrosanct and cannot be abridged or taken away by Parliament.
  • Kesavananda Bharati Case (1973): 13-judge bench (7:6 majority). Formulated Basic Structure Doctrine. Ruled Parliament can amend any part of Constitution under Art 368, but cannot alter/destroy the Basic Structure.
  • Minerva Mills Case (1980): SC reaffirmed Basic Structure; ruled Judicial Review and balance between Fundamental Rights & DPSP are basic features.
  • Maneka Gandhi Case (1978): Expanded scope of Article 21 (Personal Liberty); established “Procedure established by law” must be “due, just, fair and reasonable”.
  • SR Bommai Case (1994): Restricted arbitrary imposition of President’s Rule (Art 356); held Secularism and Federalism are basic features.
  • Indra Sawhney Case (1992) (Mandal Case): Upheld 50% cap on total reservations; excluded “creamy layer” from OBC quotas.
  • Vishaka Case (1997): Laid down Vishaka Guidelines to prevent sexual harassment of women at workplace (led to POSH Act 2013).
  • Puttaswamy Case (2017): 9-judge bench unanimously held Right to Privacy is a Fundamental Right under Article 21.
