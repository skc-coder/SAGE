Physics Cheat Sheet & Formula Master

Comprehensive reference guide containing all essential definitions, principles, SI units, and quantitative formulas across all major physics domains for competitive examinations.

1. Physical Quantities, Kinematics & Mechanics

Core Definitions

  • Scalar Quantities: Physical quantities possessing magnitude only and no directional dependence (e.g., Mass, Speed, Volume, Energy, Work, Time, Power).
  • Vector Quantities: Physical quantities possessing both magnitude and specific direction that obey the vector addition triangle law (e.g., Displacement, Velocity, Acceleration, Force, Linear Momentum, Torque).
  • Distance (s): Total scalar path length traversed by an object between initial and final states (s≥0).
  • Displacement (x): Shortest directed vector path from initial position x1​ to final position x2​. ∥x∥≤Distance
  • Speed (v): Time rate of scalar distance coverage: v=ΔtΔs​
  • Velocity (v): Time rate of change of position vector: v=dtdx​
  • Harmonic Average Speed: If equal distances s are traversed at speeds v1​ and v2​, the average speed is: vavg​=v1​+v2​2v1​v2​​
  • Relative Velocity:
    • Same direction: vrel​=v1​−v2​
    • Opposite direction: vrel​=v1​+v2​
  • Acceleration (a): Time rate of change of velocity: a=dtdv​(SI Unit: m/s2)

Kinematic Equations of Motion (Constant Acceleration a)

  1. Linear Speed-Time Relation: v=u+at
  2. Position-Time Relation: s=ut+21​at2
  3. Position-Velocity Relation: v2=u2+2as
  4. Distance Traversed in the n-th Second: sn​=u+2a​(2n−1)

Note for motion under gravity: Replace a with +g (downward) or −g (upward), where g≈9.8 m/s2.

2. Dynamics, Projectile Motion & Work-Energy-Power

Projectile Motion Formulas

For projection angle θ with initial speed u:

  • Time of Flight (T): T=g2usinθ​
  • Maximum Height (H): H=2gu2sin2θ​
  • Horizontal Range (R): R=gu2sin(2θ)​ (Max range occurs at θ=45∘, and identical range occurs at complementary angles θ and 90∘−θ.)

Newton’s Laws of Motion & Momentum

  • First Law (Law of Inertia): A body remains at rest or in uniform linear motion unless compelled by a net external force. Mass measures quantitative inertia.
  • Second Law: F=dtdp​​=ma(1 N=105 dynes)
  • Third Law: To every action force, there is an equal and opposite reaction force acting on distinct bodies (FAB​=−FBA​).
  • Linear Momentum (p​): p​=mv (SI Unit: kg⋅m/s).
  • Conservation of Linear Momentum: In an isolated system (Fext​=0): m1​u1​+m2​u2​=m1​v1​+m2​v2​
  • Impulse (I): Product of a large force and short time interval: I=FΔt=Δp​

Friction & Centripetal Acceleration

  • Frictional Force: Opposes relative interfacial motion: fmax​=μN μs​>μk​>μr​(Static>Kinetic>Rolling)
  • Centripetal Acceleration: ac​=rv2​=ω2r
  • Centripetal Force: Fc​=rmv2​

Work, Energy & Power

  • Work (W): Scalar product of force and displacement: W=F⋅s=Fscosθ(SI Unit: Joule, 1 J=107 ergs)
  • Kinetic Energy (K): Energy due to motion: K=21​mv2=2mp2​
  • Gravitational Potential Energy (U): U=mgh
  • Power (P): Rate of work execution: P=dtdW​=F⋅v(SI Unit: Watt, 1 HP=746 W)
  • Commercial Electrical Energy: 1 kWh=3.6×106 J

3. Rotational Motion & Gravitation

Rotational Dynamics

  • Angular Velocity (ω): ω=dtdθ​=T2π​=2πf.
  • Linear vs Angular Relation: v=rω.
  • Moment of Inertia (I): I=∑mi​ri2​=MK2
    • Solid Sphere: I=52​MR2
    • Circular Ring / Thin Shell: I=MR2
    • Circular Disc / Solid Cylinder: I=21​MR2
  • Angular Momentum (L): L=Iω=rpsinθ
  • Torque (τ): τ=Iα=r×F

Gravitation & Celestial Mechanics

  • Newton’s Law of Gravitation: F=Gr2m1​m2​​(G≈6.67×10−11 N⋅m2/kg2)
  • Acceleration due to Gravity (g): g=R2GM​≈9.8 m/s2
    • Altitude h: g′=g(1−R2h​)
    • Depth d: g′=g(1−Rd​) (At Earth’s center, g′=0).
    • Earth Latitude λ: g′=g−Rω2cos2λ (Max at poles, min at equator).
  • Orbital Velocity (vo​): vo​=RGM​​=gR​≈7.92 km/s
  • Escape Velocity (ve​): ve​=R2GM​​=2gR​=2​vo​≈11.2 km/s
  • Geostationary Satellite: Altitude h≈36,000 km, Time Period T=24 hours, rotates West to East in equatorial plane.
  • Kepler’s Laws:
    1. Elliptical Orbits with Sun at one focus.
    2. Areal Velocity conservation (dtdA​=constant).
    3. Harmonic Law: T2∝a3.

4. Properties of Matter & Fluid Mechanics

Elasticity & Hydrostatics

  • Stress & Strain: Stress=AreaForce​,Strain=LΔL​
  • Hooke’s Law: Stress∝Strain (up to elastic limit). Y=Longitudinal StrainLongitudinal Stress​
  • Fluid Pressure (P): P=AF​(SI Unit: Pascal, 1 Pa=1 N/m2,1 atm=1.013×105 Pa=760 mmHg)
  • Hydrostatic Pressure at Depth h: P=P0​+ρgh
  • Pascal’s Law: Pressure applied to enclosed incompressible fluid is transmitted undiminished in all directions (basis for hydraulic press).
  • Archimedes’ Principle & Buoyancy: FB​=Weight of displaced liquid=ρfluid​Vsubmerged​g
    • Floating condition: Weight of object=Buoyant force.
  • Density, Specific Gravity & Liquid Mixtures: Density d=VolumeMass​,Relative Density=dwater at 4∘C​dsubstance​​
    • Equal Volume Mixture (V1​=V2​): D=2d1​+d2​​ (Arithmetic Mean)
    • Equal Mass Mixture (m1​=m2​): D=d1​+d2​2d1​d2​​ (Harmonic Mean)

Hydrodynamics & Surface Dynamics

  • Equation of Continuity: A1​v1​=A2​v2​=constant
  • Bernoulli’s Theorem: For streamlined flow of an ideal fluid: P+21​ρv2+ρgh=constant
  • Surface Tension (T): Liquid surface contractive force per unit length: T=LF​(SI Unit: N/m)
  • Capillary Rise (h): h=rρg2Tcosθ​ (Water rises in glass capillary (θ<90∘), mercury depresses (θ>90∘).)
  • Stokes’ Law & Terminal Velocity: Fv​=6πηrv vT​=9η2r2(ρ−σ)g​

5. Heat, Thermodynamics & Waves

Temperature Scales & Thermal Physics

  • Temperature Scale Conversion: 5C​=9F−32​=4R​=5K−273.15​
    • Absolute Zero: 0 K=−273.15∘C.
    • Equal Celsius and Fahrenheit reading: −40∘C=−40∘F.
  • Heat Capacity & Specific Heat: Q=mcΔT(cwater​=1 cal/g∘C=4186 J/kg⋅K)
  • Latent Heat (L): Q=mL(Lfusion, ice​=80 cal/g,Lvaporization, water​=540 cal/g)
  • Thermal Expansion: ΔL=αLΔT,ΔA=βAΔT,ΔV=γVΔT α:β:γ=1:2:3

Thermodynamics & Waves

  • First Law of Thermodynamics: ΔQ=ΔU+W
  • Second Law & Entropy: Heat cannot spontaneously flow from a colder body to a hotter body without external work.
  • Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM): x(t)=Asin(ωt+ϕ)
    • Simple Pendulum Time Period: T=2πgL​​
  • Wave Velocity (v): v=fλ=Tλ​
  • Speed of Sound: v=ργP​​=MγRT​​ (Sound requires a physical medium; vsolid​>vliquid​>vgas​. In air at 0∘C, v≈332 m/s.)

6. Optics & Light

Reflection & Refraction

  • Laws of Reflection: θi​=θr​.
  • Mirror Formula & Magnification: f1​=v1​+u1​,m=−uv​=ho​hi​​
    • Focal length: f=2R​ (Concave: f<0, Convex: f>0).
  • Snell’s Law of Refraction: μ=sinrsini​=vc​
  • Critical Angle (θc​) & Total Internal Reflection (TIR): sinθc​=μ1​ (Condition for TIR: Light travels from denser to rarer medium with i>θc​. Applications: Optical fibers, mirages, diamond sparkle.)
  • Lens Formula & Power: f1​=v1​−u1​,P=f (meters)1​(Unit: Dioptre, D)

Human Eye Defects & Corrections

  • Myopia (Short-sightedness): Image forms in front of retina. Corrected using Concave Lens (P<0).
  • Hypermetropia (Far-sightedness): Image forms behind retina. Corrected using Convex Lens (P>0).
  • Presbyopia: Age-related loss of accommodation. Corrected using Bifocal Lens.
  • Astigmatism: Asymmetrical cornea curvature. Corrected using Cylindrical Lens.
