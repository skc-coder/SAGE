Metals and Non-Metals

Exhaustive chapter notes covering Physical & Chemical Properties of Metals vs Non-Metals, Reactivity Series, Amphoteric Oxides, Ionic vs Covalent Bonding, Metallurgy & Ore Extraction, Corrosion & Alloy Formations.

2. Chemical Properties & Reactivity Series

A. Reaction of Metals with Oxygen (Air)

Metal+Oxygen→Metal Oxide

  • Most metal oxides are Basic in nature (turn red litmus blue). Metallic oxides react with water to form alkalis (Na2​O+H2​O→2NaOH).
  • Amphoteric Oxides: Metal oxides that react with BOTH acids and bases to produce salt and water. Aluminium Oxide: Al2​O3​∣Zinc Oxide: ZnO
    • Reaction with Acid: Al2​O3​+6HCl→2AlCl3​+3H2​O
    • Reaction with Base: Al2​O3​+2NaOH→Sodium Aluminate2NaAlO2​​​+H2​O
  • Anodising: Process of forming a thick protective oxide layer on Aluminium using electrolysis to prevent corrosion.

4. Metallurgy & Important Ores

Processes used for extraction of pure metals from their ores.

  • Gangue: Earthy impurities (sand, soil, clay) associated with mined ore.
  • Roasting: Heating Sulphide ores in the presence of excess air to convert into oxides. 2ZnS+3O2​Δ​2ZnO+2SO2​↑
  • Calcination: Heating Carbonate ores in limited / absence of air to convert into oxides. ZnCO3​Δ​ZnO+CO2​↑
  • Thermite Reaction: Reduction of metal oxides using Aluminium powder as reducing agent (highly exothermic, metal produced in molten state). Fe2​O3​(s)+2Al(s)→2Fe(l)+Al2​O3​(s)+Heat
    • Application: Used to join railway tracks and cracked machine parts.

6. Uses & Applications of Specific Special Metals

A. Mercury (Hg)

  • Barometers & Manometers: Used in pressure measurement due to its high density (13.53 g/cm3) and uniform thermal expansion.
  • Dental Amalgam Fillings: Mixed with silver, tin, and copper powders to make durable dental cavity fillings.
  • Fluorescent Lighting: Mercury vapor in fluorescent tubes produces ultraviolet light when excited by electric current, which phosphor coatings then convert to visible light.
  • Industrial Electrolysis: Historically used as a liquid cathode in the Chlor-Alkali process to produce chlorine gas and sodium hydroxide (NaOH).
  • Gold and Silver Extraction: Used in artisanal mining to amalgamate with fine gold particles, separating gold from soil and rock.

B. Gallium (Ga)

  • Semiconductors & Microelectronics: Primary raw material for Gallium Arsenide (GaAs) and Gallium Nitride (GaN), which power high-speed transistors, solar panels, power supplies, and blue/UV LEDs.
  • Non-Toxic Thermometers: Alloyed with indium and tin to form Galinstan, a liquid alloy at room temperature used as a safe, non-toxic replacement for mercury in medical thermometers.
  • Thermal Interface Materials: Liquid gallium alloys are used in high-performance computer cooling (liquid metal compounds) due to high thermal conductivity.
  • Flexible Electronics & Soft Robotics: Used to print flexible, self-healing conductive tracks for wearable circuits.

C. Cesium (Cs)

  • Atomic Clocks: Powers the world’s primary time standards. The official SI definition of 1 second is based on 9,192,631,770 radiation cycles of the Cesium-133 atom.
  • Oil & Gas Drilling Fluids: Cesium formate (HCOOCs) brine is used as a heavy, dense drilling fluid to lubricate drill bits and prevent high-pressure blowout gas leaks in deep wells.
  • Photoelectric Cells & Night Vision: Low ionization energy allows cesium to emit electrons easily when hit by light, making it useful in photomultiplier tubes and night-vision equipment.
  • Ion Thrusters: Used as a propellant in spacecraft ion propulsion systems because its low ionization energy makes it easy to ionize into beam thrust.

8. Hard Exam PYQs & Advanced Practice Questions

Q6 (CDS 2 2025 - Electrolysis of Molten Compound)

Question: Which one of the following metals is widely extracted by the electrolysis of its molten compound? (a) Cu | (b) Au | (c) Sn | (d) Na

  • Answer: (d) Na (Sodium)
  • Exhaustive Explanation & Intuition:
    • Reactivity & Extraction Rule: Metals high up in the reactivity series (K,Na,Ca,Mg,Al) have extremely high affinity for oxygen and chlorine, far higher than carbon. Thus, carbon cannot reduce their oxides or salts.
    • Why Molten Electrolysis (Down’s Process)?
      • Highly reactive metals are extracted via electrolytic reduction of their molten (fused) chlorides or oxides.
      • Sodium is extracted by the electrolysis of molten Sodium Chloride (NaCl) (often mixed with CaCl2​ to lower melting point from 801∘C to ∼600∘C).
      • Reactions during electrolysis:
        • Cathode (-): Na++e−→Na (s) (Sodium metal deposited at cathode)
        • Anode (+): 2Cl−→Cl2​↑+2e− (Chlorine gas liberated at anode)
    • Why aqueous solution cannot be used for Sodium? If aqueous NaCl solution is electrolyzed, H+ ions from water are reduced at cathode instead of Na+ ions because H+ has lower reduction potential, liberating H2​ gas instead of sodium metal!
    • Elimination of Other Options:
      • Cu (Copper): Low reactivity metal. Extracted by self-reduction of sulphide ore (Cu2​S) or pyrometallurgy; purified by electrolytic refining.
      • Au (Gold): Unreactive noble metal, found free in nature. Extracted by physical panning or cyanide leaching.
      • Sn (Tin): Moderate reactivity metal. Extracted by reduction of its oxide ore Cassiterite (SnO2​) using carbon (coke).

🎯 Hard Variations (Practice Questions):

  • Variation 1 (Aqueous vs Molten Product Conflict): What products are formed at the cathode and anode respectively when aqueous NaCl solution is electrolyzed instead of molten NaCl?
    • Solution: Cathode: H2​(g); Anode: Cl2​(g); Solution: NaOH(aq) (Chlor-Alkali Process). Sodium metal is NOT produced in water!
  • Variation 2 (Flux Role in Down’s Cell): Why is Calcium Chloride (CaCl2​) added to Sodium Chloride (NaCl) during commercial extraction of sodium metal?
    • Solution: Pure NaCl melts at a high temperature (801∘C), near the boiling point of sodium metal. CaCl2​ acts as a flux to lower the melting point mixture to ∼600∘C, saving energy and preventing sodium vaporization.
  • Variation 3 (Magnesium Molten Electrolysis): What metal is obtained at the cathode during the electrolysis of molten Magnesium Chloride (MgCl2​)?
    • Solution: Magnesium metal (Mg) (Mg2++2e−→Mg(s)).
  • Variation 4 (Alumina Electrolysis Electrode): During the Hall-Héroult electrolytic reduction of molten Alumina (Al2​O3​), why must the graphite anode rods be replaced periodically?
    • Solution: Oxygen gas liberated at the anode (2O2−→O2​+4e−) reacts with the hot carbon anode to form CO and CO2​ gas, slowly burning away the graphite rods!

Q7 (CDS 2 2025 / NDA - Corrosion of Copper)

Question: When copper reacts with moist carbon dioxide (CO2​) in air, it forms a green coating of which one of the following compounds? (a) Cupric carbonate | (b) Cuprous oxide | (c) Cupric oxide | (d) Copper sulphate

  • Answer: (a) Cupric carbonate (Basic Copper Carbonate: CuCO3​⋅Cu(OH)2​)
  • Exhaustive Explanation & Intuition:
    • Corrosion Mechanism: Copper slowly reacts with moist CO2​, O2​, and water vapor present in the atmosphere to lose its shiny brown luster and gain a green coat.
    • Chemical Reaction: 2Cu(s)+H2​O(l)+CO2​(g)+O2​(g)→Basic Copper Carbonate / Cupric Carbonate (Green)CuCO3​⋅Cu(OH)2​(s)​​
    • Key Distinctions for Exams:
      • Iron Rusting: Reddish-brown hydrated ferric oxide (Fe2​O3​⋅xH2​O).
      • Silver Tarnishing: Black coating of Silver Sulphide (Ag2​S) from atmospheric H2​S.
      • Copper Corrosion: Green coating of Basic Cupric Carbonate (CuCO3​⋅Cu(OH)2​).

🎯 Hard Variations (Practice Questions):

  • Variation 1 (Silver Tarnishing Gas): Silver articles turn black when exposed to air for a long time. This is due to the formation of a coating of:
    • (a) Ag2​O | (b) Ag2​S | (c) AgCN | (d) AgNO3​
    • Solution: (b) Ag2​S (Silver Sulphide) due to reaction with hydrogen sulphide gas (H2​S) in air.
  • Variation 2 (Verdigris Mineral Composition): What is the common name and chemical formula of the green patina formed on old copper monuments (like the Statue of Liberty)?
    • Solution: Verdigris / Patina, composed of Basic Copper Carbonate (CuCO3​⋅Cu(OH)2​) or Basic Copper Sulphate in industrial areas.
  • Variation 3 (Sacrificial Protection / Galvanization): Iron sheets are coated with Zinc to prevent rusting. Even if the zinc coating is scratched, iron does not rust. Why?
    • Solution: Zinc is more reactive than Iron (EZn2+/Zn∘​<EFe2+/Fe∘​). Zinc oxidizes preferentially, acting as a sacrificial anode to protect the underlying iron.
  • Variation 4 (Rusting Conditions Drill): Why does iron NOT rust in boiled distilled water sealed under a layer of oil?
    • Solution: Boiling removes dissolved oxygen gas from water, and oil prevents atmospheric oxygen from re-dissolving. Rusting requires BOTH oxygen AND moisture simultaneously!

Q8 (NDA 1 2021 - Electrolytic Refining of Copper)

Question: In electrolytic refining of copper, the electrolyte is a solution of: (a) acidified copper chloride | (b) acidified copper sulphate | (c) potassium chloride | (d) sodium sulphate

  • Answer: (b) acidified copper sulphate (CuSO4​+H2​SO4​)
  • Exhaustive Explanation & Intuition:
    • Setup for Electrolytic Refining:
      • Anode (+): Impure copper block (dissolves into solution).
      • Cathode (-): Thin strip of pure copper metal (pure copper deposits here).
      • Electrolyte: Aqueous solution of Copper Sulphate (CuSO4​) acidified with dilute Sulphuric Acid (H2​SO4​) to improve electrical conductivity.
    • Process:
      • On passing electric current, pure metal from anode dissolves into electrolyte, and an equivalent amount of pure copper metal from electrolyte is deposited at cathode:
        • Anode Reaction: Cu(impure)→Cu2++2e−
        • Cathode Reaction: Cu2++2e−→Cu(pure)
      • Soluble impurities go into solution; insoluble impurities settle down at bottom of anode as Anode Mud (contains valuable noble metals like Au,Ag,Pt).

🎯 Hard Variations (Practice Questions):

  • Variation 1 (Anode Mud Composition): In the electrolytic refining of Copper, which of the following metals are recovered from the ‘Anode Mud’?
    • (a) Na and K | (b) Fe and Zn | (c) Au and Ag | (d) Al and Mg
    • Solution: (c) Au (Gold) and Ag (Silver) (along with Platinum). Noble metals do not oxidize at anode potential and settle to the bottom.
  • Variation 2 (Anode vs Cathode Mass Changes): During electrolytic refining of impure copper for 2 hours, what happens to the mass of the anode and cathode?
    • Solution: Mass of Anode decreases (impure copper dissolves) and mass of Cathode increases by an equal mass of pure copper deposited.
  • Variation 3 (Soluble Impurities Behavior): What happens to reactive metal impurities like Zinc and Iron present in crude copper during electrolytic refining?
    • Solution: Zinc and Iron oxidize into the electrolyte solution as Zn2+ and Fe2+ ions, but remain in solution because Cu2+ ions have a higher reduction potential and deposit preferentially at the cathode.
  • Variation 4 (Refining Acid Role): Why is dilute Sulphuric Acid (H2​SO4​) added to the CuSO4​ electrolyte bath?
    • Solution: Dilute H2​SO4​ increases ionic concentration/conductivity of the solution and prevents hydrolysis of CuSO4​ into basic copper hydroxide precipitate.

Q9 (CDS Hard Practice - Oxides Classification)

Question: Which one of the following oxides is a neutral oxide? (a) CO | (b) CO2​ | (c) Na2​O | (d) MgO

  • Answer: (a) CO (Carbon Monoxide)
  • Exhaustive Explanation & Intuition:
    • Neutral Oxides Definition: Non-metal oxides that show neither acidic nor basic properties. They do not react with acids or bases, and turn neither red nor blue litmus paper.
    • Complete Classification of Common Oxides:
      • Neutral Oxides (Remember these 4 Key Examples): Carbon Monoxide (CO), Nitric Oxide (NO), Nitrous Oxide (N2​O - laughing gas), and Water (H2​O).
      • Acidic Oxides: Non-metal oxides (CO2​, SO2​, SO3​, NO2​, P4​O10​). React with water to form acids (CO2​+H2​O→H2​CO3​).
      • Basic Oxides: Metallic oxides of Group 1 & 2 metals (Na2​O, K2​O, CaO, MgO). React with water to form alkalis (Na2​O+H2​O→2NaOH).
      • Amphoteric Oxides: Aluminium oxide (Al2​O3​) and Zinc oxide (ZnO) — react with both acids and bases.

🎯 Hard Variations (Practice Questions):

  • Variation 1 (Nitrogen Oxide Neutral Identification): Which of the following oxides of nitrogen is neutral to litmus?
    • (a) N2​O5​ | (b) NO2​ | (c) N2​O | (d) N2​O3​
    • Solution: (c) N2​O (Nitrous oxide) (and NO). NO2​,N2​O3​,N2​O5​ are acidic oxides forming nitrous/nitric acids.
  • Variation 2 (Litmus Behavior Drill): A gas X dissolves in water to turn blue litmus paper red. Gas Y dissolves in water but causes NO change in red or blue litmus paper. Identify X and Y.
    • Solution: X=CO2​ or SO2​ (Acidic oxide); Y=CO or N2​O (Neutral oxide).
  • Variation 3 (Mixed Acid Anhydride): Which non-metal oxide is called a ‘mixed acid anhydride’ because it reacts with water to form a mixture of TWO acids (HNO2​ and HNO3​)?
    • Solution: Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2​) (2NO2​+H2​O→HNO2​+HNO3​).
  • Variation 4 (Peroxide vs Normal Oxide Reaction): What gas is liberated when Sodium Peroxide (Na2​O2​) reacts with dilute Sulphuric Acid?
    • Solution: Hydrogen Peroxide (H2​O2​) (Na2​O2​+H2​SO4​→Na2​SO4​+H2​O2​). (Normal Na2​O gives water).

Q10 (NDA 1 2024 - Highest Melting Point Oxide)

Question: Which one among the following oxides has the highest melting point? (a) Na2​O | (b) MgO | (c) Fe2​O3​ | (d) CuO

  • Answer: (b) MgO (Magnesium Oxide - Melting Point: ≈2852∘C)
  • Exhaustive Explanation & First-Principles Derivation:
    • Lattice Energy Principle: Melting point of ionic compounds is directly proportional to their Lattice Energy (U). U∝r++r−∣z+⋅z−∣​ where z+ and z− are the charges of the cation and anion, and r++r− is the interionic distance.
    • Comparing Ionic Charges:
      • In MgO: Cation is Mg2+, Anion is O2−. Charge product =∣+2×−2∣=4.
      • In Na2​O: Cation is Na+, Anion is O2−. Charge product =∣+1×−2∣=2.
      • In CuO: Cation is Cu2+, Anion is O2−. Charge product =∣+2×−2∣=4, BUT Cu2+ has a pseudo-noble gas configuration leading to higher covalent character (Fajans’ Rule) which lowers its ionic lattice energy compared to pure alkaline earth oxide MgO.
      • In Fe2​O3​: High covalent character and lower symmetry than rock-salt MgO.
    • Interionic Distance Effect: Mg2+ has a very small ionic radius (∼72 pm), leading to an extremely high charge-to-size ratio and extraordinarily strong electrostatic attractions.
    • Industrial Significance: MgO is used as a refractory lining for steel furnaces, kilns, and crucibles because it withstands extreme temperatures without melting or decomposing!

🎯 Hard Variations (Practice Questions):

  • Variation 1 (Order of Melting Points): Arrange NaCl, MgO, and CaO in increasing order of melting points.
    • Solution: NaCl(801∘C)<CaO(2572∘C)<MgO(2852∘C). Mg2+ is smaller than Ca2+, so MgO has higher lattice energy than CaO.
  • Variation 2 (Refractory Material Application): Why is Magnesium Oxide (MgO) preferred over Sodium Oxide (Na2​O) for furnace brick manufacturing?
    • Solution: MgO has a higher lattice energy due to divalent Mg2+ ions, giving it a much higher melting point (2852∘C) and chemical stability against basic slag.
  • Variation 3 (Fajans’ Rule Contrast): Why does MgO (2852∘C) have a significantly higher melting point than CuO (1326∘C), even though both cations carry a +2 charge?
    • Solution: Cu2+ (3d9) has a non-noble gas electron configuration, exerting higher polarizing power on O2−, imparting significant covalent character to CuO (lowering MP). Mg2+ (2s22p6) is a rigid noble-gas cation forming pure ionic bonds.
  • Variation 4 (Lattice Energy Formula Drill): If the distance between ions is halved and charges on both ions are doubled, by what factor does lattice energy increase?
    • Solution: U∝rz1​z2​​→New U=(r/2)(2z1​)(2z2​)​=8×U. Lattice energy increases by a factor of 8.

Q11 (CAPF 2021 - Reaction of Oxide with Sodium Hydroxide)

Question: Which one of the following metal oxides does not usually react with sodium hydroxide (NaOH)? (a) Aluminium oxide | (b) Calcium oxide | (c) Copper oxide | (d) Potassium oxide

  • Answer: (b) Calcium oxide (CaO) / (c) Copper oxide / (d) Potassium oxide — Primary Correct Base-Base Exclusion: CaO & K2​O
  • Exhaustive Explanation & Intuition:
    • Acid-Base Reaction Rule:
      • Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH) is a strong base / alkali.
      • A base will react with Acidic Oxides (non-metal oxides) and Amphoteric Oxides (oxides that can act as acids).
      • A base will NOT react with Basic Oxides (like CaO,K2​O,Na2​O,MgO) because Base + Base = No Reaction!
    • Analyzing the Options:
      • Al2​O3​ (Aluminium Oxide): AMPHOTERIC OXIDE. Reacts readily with NaOH to form sodium aluminate and water: Al2​O3​+2NaOH→Sodium Aluminate2NaAlO2​​​+H2​O
      • CaO (Calcium Oxide): STRONGLY BASIC OXIDE. Calcium is a Group 2 alkaline earth metal. Basic oxides do NOT react with basic NaOH.
      • ZnO & PbO (Other Amphoteric Oxides): React with NaOH to form Zincates (Na2​ZnO2​) and Plumbites (Na2​PbO2​).

🎯 Hard Variations (Practice Questions):

  • Variation 1 (Amphoteric Identification): Which oxide dissolves in both concentrated HCl and concentrated NaOH?
    • (a) CaO | (b) ZnO | (c) Na2​O | (d) CuO
    • Solution: (b) ZnO. Zinc oxide is amphoteric (ZnO+2NaOH→Na2​ZnO2​+H2​O).
  • Variation 2 (Aluminate Salt Formula): What is the chemical formula of the salt formed when Aluminium metal dissolves in aqueous Sodium Hydroxide?
    • Solution: NaAlO2​ (Sodium Metaluminate) or Na[Al(OH)4​]. Reaction: 2Al+2NaOH+2H2​O→2NaAlO2​+3H2​↑.
  • Variation 3 (Non-metal Oxide Contrast): What happens when CO2​ gas is bubbled through aqueous NaOH?
    • Solution: Acidic non-metal oxide CO2​ reacts with basic NaOH to form Sodium Carbonate salt: 2NaOH+CO2​→Na2​CO3​+H2​O.
  • Variation 4 (Oxide Nature Sorting): Classify CrO, Cr2​O3​, and CrO3​ as acidic, basic, or amphoteric.
    • Solution: Low oxidation state CrO(+2) is Basic; intermediate Cr2​O3​(+3) is Amphoteric; high oxidation state CrO3​(+6) is Acidic!

Q12 (NDA 2 2023 - Extraction of Metals Using Carbon Reduction)

Question: Which one of the following metals can be extracted using carbon as a reducing agent? (a) Zinc | (b) Silver | (c) Gold | (d) Aluminium

  • Answer: (a) Zinc (Zn)
  • Exhaustive Explanation & Extraction Hierarchy:
    • Metallurgical Reduction Rules Based on Reactivity:
      1. High Reactivity Metals (K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al):
        • Affinity for oxygen is higher than carbon. Carbon CANNOT reduce their oxides.
        • Extracted by Electrolytic Reduction of molten salts (Down’s / Hall-Héroult process).
      2. Middle Reactivity Metals (Zn, Fe, Pb, Cu):
        • Moderate affinity for oxygen. Extracted by Carbon (Coke) or Carbon Monoxide (CO) Reduction of their oxides in a furnace!
        • Reaction for Zinc: ZnO(s)+C(s)Δ​Zn(s)+CO(g)
      3. Low Reactivity / Noble Metals (Hg, Ag, Au, Pt):
        • Oxides are unstable. Extracted by Thermal Decomposition (Auto-reduction / Heating alone) or found native in free state.
        • Reaction for Cinnabar: 2HgS+3O2​Δ​2HgO+2SO2​⟹2HgOΔ​2Hg+O2​↑.

🎯 Hard Variations (Practice Questions):

  • Variation 1 (Blast Furnace Reduction): In the extraction of Iron from Haematite (Fe2​O3​) in a blast furnace, what is the main reducing agent at temperatures around 400∘C−700∘C?
    • Solution: Carbon Monoxide (CO) (Fe2​O3​+3CO→2Fe+3CO2​).
  • Variation 2 (Thermite Reduction Agent): In the welding of railway tracks, which element is used as a reducing agent to convert Iron oxide to molten iron?
    • Solution: Aluminium powder (Al) (Fe2​O3​+2Al→2Fe(l)+Al2​O3​+Heat). This is the Thermite Reaction.
  • Variation 3 (Auto-Reduction Metal): Which of the following metals is extracted without adding any external reducing agent like Coke or Aluminium?
    • (a) Al | (b) Hg | (c) Fe | (d) Na
    • Solution: (b) Hg. Heating Cinnabar (HgS) in air converts it to HgO, which auto-decomposes to liquid Mercury upon continued heating.
  • Variation 4 (Hall-Héroult Process Role of Cryolite): Why is Cryolite (Na3​AlF6​) added during the electrolytic extraction of Aluminium from alumina (Al2​O3​)?
    • Solution: Pure Al2​O3​ has a very high melting point (2050∘C) and poor electrical conductivity. Cryolite lowers the melting point to ∼950∘C and increases electrical conductivity!
