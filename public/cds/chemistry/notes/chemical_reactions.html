Chemical Reactions & Equations

Exhaustive chapter notes covering characteristics of chemical reactions, balancing equations, types of chemical reactions (Combination, Decomposition, Displacement, Double Displacement), Exothermic vs Endothermic processes, Redox theory (Oxidation states, Oxidizing/Reducing agents), Catalysts, Corrosion, and Rancidity.

1. Basics of Chemical Reactions & Equations

  • Characteristics of a Chemical Reaction:

    1. Change in state (e.g., liquid to gas).
    2. Change in color (e.g., copper sulphate turning pale green with iron).
    3. Evolution of a gas (e.g., H2​↑,CO2​↑,NO2​↑).
    4. Change in temperature (Exothermic / Endothermic).
    5. Formation of a precipitate (↓).

  • State Symbols in Equations:

    • (s) = solid | (l) = liquid | (g) = gas / vapor | (aq) = aqueous solution (dissolved in water)
    • ^ / \uparrow = gas evolved | \downarrow = precipitate formed | \Delta = heat supplied

  • Catalysts: Substances that alter the rate of a chemical reaction without undergoing any permanent chemical change themselves.

    • Positive Catalyst: Increases reaction rate by lowering activation energy (e.g., Nickel Ni in hydrogenation of oils; V2​O5​ in Contact Process; MnO2​ in decomposition of KClO3​).
    • Negative Catalyst / Inhibitor: Decreases reaction rate (e.g., Glycerol / Acetanilide retards decomposition of H2​O2​).

2. Types of Chemical Reactions

A. Combination Reaction (A+B→C)

Two or more reactants combine to form a single product.

  1. Burning of Magnesium Ribbon: 2Mg(s)+O2​(g)→2MgO(s)
    • Observations: Burns with a dazzling white flame forming a white powder (MgO).
    • Precaution: Clean ribbon with sandpaper to remove protective layer of basic magnesium carbonate.
  2. Slaking of Lime (Highly Exothermic): QuicklimeCaO(s)​​+H2​O(l)→Slaked LimeCa(OH)2​(aq)​​+Heat
    • White-washing Walls: Ca(OH)2​ applied on walls reacts slowly with atmospheric CO2​ over 2-3 days to form a shiny layer of Calcium Carbonate (CaCO3​): Ca(OH)2​(aq)+CO2​(g)→CaCO3​(s)+H2​O(l)

B. Decomposition Reaction (A→B+C)

A single compound breaks down into two or more simpler substances. Requires energy in the form of Heat (Thermal), Light (Photolytic), or Electricity (Electrolytic).

  1. Thermal Decomposition of Ferrous Sulphate: 2FeSO4​⋅7H2​OΔ​Ferric Oxide (Red-Brown)Fe2​O3​(s)​​+Pungent suffocating smell of burning sulphurSO2​(g)+SO3​(g)​​
    • Green ferrous sulphate crystals lose water of crystallization and turn white, then decompose to reddish-brown Fe2​O3​.
  2. Thermal Decomposition of Lead Nitrate: 2Pb(NO3​)2​(s)Δ​2PbO(s)+Brown Fumes4NO2​(g)​​+O2​(g)
    • Crucial Exam Point: Emission of brown fumes indicates Nitrogen Dioxide (NO2​) gas! PbO residue is yellow.
  3. Photolytic Decomposition of Silver Halides (Black & White Photography): 2AgCl(s)Sunlight​2Ag(s)+Cl2​(g)(White AgCl→Grey Silver) 2\text{AgBr(s)} \xrightarrow{\text{Sunlight}} 2\text{Ag(s)} + \text{Br}_2\text{(g)} \quad (\text{Used in Black & White Photography})

C. Displacement Reaction (A+BC→AC+B)

A more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its salt solution.

Fe(s)+Blue SolutionCuSO4​(aq)​​→Pale Green SolutionFeSO4​(aq)​​+Reddish-brown deposit on iron nailCu(s)​​ Zn(s)+CuSO4​(aq)→ZnSO4​(aq)+Cu(s)(Blue solution becomes colorless)

D. Double Displacement / Precipitation Reaction (AB+CD→AD+CB)

Exchange of ions between reactants to form an insoluble precipitate.

Sodium SulphateNa2​SO4​(aq)​​+Barium ChlorideBaCl2​(aq)​​→White ppt of Barium SulphateBaSO4​↓​​+2NaCl(aq) Pb(NO3​)2​(aq)+2KI(aq)→Yellow ppt of Lead IodidePbI2​↓​​+2KNO3​(aq)

4. Redox Reactions & Oxidation Numbers

Redox = Reduction + Oxidation occurring simultaneously.

Classical vs Electronic vs Oxidation Number Concept

ConceptOxidationReduction
Oxygen / HydrogenGain of Oxygen / Loss of HydrogenLoss of Oxygen / Gain of Hydrogen
Electron Transfer (OIL RIG)Oxidation Is Loss of electrons (e−)Reduction Is Gain of electrons (e−)
Oxidation Number (ON)Increase in Oxidation NumberDecrease in Oxidation Number

Agents

  • Oxidizing Agent (Oxidant): Gets reduced itself; causes oxidation in others (accepts electrons / increases ON of others).
    • Examples: KMnO4​ (strongest in acidic medium), K2​Cr2​O7​, HNO3​, O2​, Cl2​, H2​O2​.
  • Reducing Agent (Reductant): Gets oxidized itself; causes reduction in others (donates electrons / decreases ON of others).
    • Examples: H2​, C (Coke), CO, LiAlH4​, NaBH4​, Na, Zn.

Sample Redox Breakdown

CuO+H2​Δ​Cu+H2​O

  • CuO→Cu (Loss of Oxygen ⟹ Reduced; CuO is the Oxidizing Agent)
  • H2​→H2​O (Gain of Oxygen ⟹ Oxidized; H2​ is the Reducing Agent)

Rules for Determining Oxidation Numbers

  1. Free element in elemental state ON =0 (e.g., O2​,N2​,Na,Fe,S8​=0).
  2. Hydrogen ON =+1 (except in metallic hydrides like NaH,CaH2​ where ON =−1).
  3. Oxygen ON =−2 (except in peroxides like H2​O2​,Na2​O2​ where ON =−1; and OF2​ where ON =+2).
  4. Alkali Metals (Group 1) ON =+1; Alkaline Earth (Group 2) ON =+2; Fluorine ON =−1.

5. Corrosion & Rancidity

A. Corrosion of Metals

Slow degradation of metals due to chemical reaction with atmospheric gases (O2​,H2​O,CO2​,H2​S).

  1. Rusting of Iron:
    • Formula of Rust: Hydrated Ferric Oxide (Fe2​O3​⋅xH2​O) (Reddish-brown flake).
    • Essential Conditions: Both Oxygen (O2​) AND Water (H2​O) are necessary!
    • Prevention: Painting, greasing, Galvanization (coating with a thin layer of Zinc Zn), Alloying (Stainless steel =Fe+Cr+Ni+C).
  2. Tarnishing of Silver:
    • Silver articles turn black due to formation of Silver Sulphide (Ag2​S) by reacting with H2​S gas in air.
  3. Corrosion of Copper:
    • Copper forms a green coating of Basic Copper Carbonate (CuCO3​⋅Cu(OH)2​) by reacting with moist CO2​ in air.

B. Rancidity

Oxidation of fats and oils in food when exposed to air, resulting in unpleasant odor and bad taste.

  • Prevention:
    1. Packing food items in an atmosphere of unreactive Nitrogen gas (N2​) (e.g., Potato chip packets).
    2. Adding Antioxidants (e.g., BHA - Butylated Hydroxyanisole, BHT - Butylated Hydroxytoluene).
    3. Keeping food in airtight containers or refrigeration.

6. Solved PYQs (NDA / CDS / CAPF)

Q1 (NDA II 2021)

Question: Heating lead nitrate Pb(NO3​)2​ in a test tube yields brown fumes of: (a) PbO | (b) NO2​ | (c) NO | (d) N2​O

  • Answer: (b) NO2​. Reaction: 2Pb(NO3​)2​→2PbO+4NO2​↑+O2​↑.

Q2 (CAPF 2022)

Question: Which one among the following is an example of an endothermic reaction? (a) Respiration | (b) Slaking of lime | (c) Decomposition of vegetable matter | (d) Dissolution of Ammonium Chloride in water

  • Answer: (d) Dissolution of Ammonium Chloride in water.

Q3 (NDA II 2021)

Question: Which one of the following compounds is used in black and white photography? (a) AgCl | (b) AgBr | (c) AgI | (d) AgNO3​

  • Answer: (b) AgBr (and AgCl).

Q4 (NDA 1 2024)

Question: What is the oxidation state of Vanadium in V2​O5​? (a) +2 | (b) +3 | (c) +4 | (d) +5

  • Answer: (d) +5. 2x+5(−2)=0⟹2x=10⟹x=+5.
