Carbon and Its Compounds

Exhaustive chapter notes covering Catenation, Tetravalency, Allotropes of Carbon (Diamond, Graphite, Fullerene, Graphene), Hydrocarbons (Saturated vs Unsaturated), Functional Groups, Chemical Properties of Ethanol & Ethanoic Acid, and Soaps & Synthetic Detergents.

1. Versatile Nature of Carbon

Carbon (Z=6, Electronic Configuration 2,4) is a non-metal placed in Group 14 of the Periodic Table. It forms covalent bonds by sharing electrons to achieve an octet.

  • Tetravalency: Carbon has 4 valence electrons and a valency of 4. It can form covalent bonds with 4 hydrogen atoms, oxygen, nitrogen, or halogen atoms.

5. Ethanol & Ethanoic Acid

A. Ethanol (C2​H5​OH)

  • Liquid at room temp; active ingredient in alcoholic drinks. Good solvent for iodine (Tincture of Iodine), cough syrups.
  • Reaction with Sodium (Na): 2C2​H5​OH+2Na→Sodium Ethoxide2C2​H5​ONa​​+H2​↑
  • Dehydration Reaction (Formation of Ethene): C2​H5​OHConc. H2​SO4​,443 K​CH2​=CH2​+H2​O (Concentrated Sulphuric Acid acts as a Dehydrating Agent).

B. Ethanoic Acid (CH3​COOH)

  • Commonly called Acetic Acid. A 5−8% solution in water is called Vinegar.
  • Glacial Acetic Acid: Pure ethanoic acid freezes at 16.6∘C (290 K) forming ice-like crystals.
  • Esterification Reaction (Sweet Fruity Smell): CH3​COOH+C2​H5​OHConc. H2​SO4​​Ethyl Ethanoate (Ester)CH3​COOC2​H5​​​+H2​O
    • Saponification: Alkaline hydrolysis of esters with NaOH to yield soap and alcohol.

6. Soaps and Synthetic Detergents

A. Cleansing Action of Soap (Micelle Formation)

A soap molecule is a Sodium or Potassium salt of long-chain carboxylic acids (e.g., Sodium Stearate C17​H35​COONa).

              HYDROPHOBIC TAIL                        HYDROPHILIC HEAD
         (Long Hydrocarbon Chain)                      (Ionic -COONa+)
       Insoluble in water / Soluble in oil           Soluble in water
  ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ (O=C-O- Na+)
  • Micelle Structure: In water, soap molecules aggregate into spherical clusters called Micelles. The hydrophobic hydrocarbon tails cluster inside towards the oily dirt particle, while the hydrophilic ionic heads face outward towards water.
  • Cloudy Soap Solution: Soap solution appears cloudy because soap micelles are large enough to scatter light (Tyndall Effect).

7. Qualitative Analysis: Lassaigne’s Test for Organic Elements

Lassaigne’s Test (Sodium Fusion Test) is used to detect extra elements (Nitrogen, Sulphur, and Halogens: Chlorine, Bromine, Iodine) in an organic compound.

  • Principle: The organic compound is fused with metallic Sodium (Na) to convert covalently bonded heteroatoms into water-soluble ionic sodium salts: Na+C+NΔ​Sodium Cyanide (for N)NaCN​​ 2Na+SΔ​Sodium Sulphide (for S)Na2​S​​ Na+XΔ​Sodium Halide (X = Cl, Br, I)NaX​​
  • Detection:
    • Nitrogen: Sodium fusion extract boiled with FeSO4​ and FeCl3​ forms Prussian Blue color (Fe4​[Fe(CN)6​]3​).
    • Sulphur: Gives violet color with Sodium Nitroprusside Na2​[Fe(CN)5​NO].
    • Halogens: Precipitate with Silver Nitrate (AgNO3​).
  • CRITICAL EXAM HOOK - What CANNOT be detected by Lassaigne’s Test?
    • Fluorine (F): Sodium Fluoride (NaF) is extremely stable and insoluble/does not yield characteristic test precipitate.
    • Carbon & Hydrogen (C, H): Detected by heating with Copper(II) Oxide (CuO), NOT by Lassaigne’s test.

8. Solved PYQs (NDA / CDS / CAPF)

Q1 (Lassaigne’s Test Element Detection)

Question: Which one of the following elements CANNOT be detected by “Lassaigne’s test”? (a) Fluorine (F) / Carbon (C) | (b) Chlorine (Cl) | (c) Iodine (I) | (d) Sulphur (S)

  • Answer: Fluorine (F) (or C/H directly). Halogens (Cl, Br, I), Nitrogen (N), and Sulphur (S) ARE detected by Lassaigne’s test.

Q2 (NDA 2 2021)

Question: What is the total number of covalent bonds in methanol (CH3​OH)? (a) 3 | (b) 4 | (c) 5 | (d) 6

  • Answer: (c) 5. (3 C-H bonds, 1 C-O bond, 1 O-H bond =5 covalent bonds).

Q3 (NDA II 2019)

Question: A sample of soft soap contains: (a) Caesium | (b) Potassium | (c) Calcium | (d) Magnesium

  • Answer: (b) Potassium. Soft soaps use KOH.

Q4 (CAPF 2022)

Question: Hydrogenation of alkenes is carried out in the presence of which catalyst? (a) Nickel (Ni) or Palladium (Pd) | (b) Iron (Fe) | (c) Platinum (Pt) only | (d) Zinc (Zn)

  • Answer: (a) Nickel (Ni) or Palladium (Pd).
