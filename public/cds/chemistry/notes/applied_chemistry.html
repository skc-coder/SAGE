Applied & Industrial Chemistry

Exhaustive chapter notes covering Hardness of Water (Temporary vs Permanent Removal), Glass Manufacturing, Portland Cement, Industrial Fertilizers (N-P-K), Explosives (Gunpowder, TNT, RDX, Dynamite), Paints & Binders, and Airbag Chemistry.

1. Hardness of Water & Treatment Methods

Hard water does not produce lather easily with soap and forms scum. Caused by dissolved Bicarbonates, Chlorides, and Sulphates of Calcium (Ca2+) and Magnesium (Mg2+).

Type of HardnessCausing SaltsTreatment / Removal MethodMemory Hook / Exam Shortcut
Temporary HardnessCalcium Bicarbonate Ca(HCO3​)2​
Magnesium Bicarbonate Mg(HCO3​)2​		1. Boiling: Converts soluble bicarbonates to insoluble carbonates (Ca(HCO3​)2​Δ​CaCO3​↓+H2​O+CO2​↑).
2. Clark’s Method: Adding calculated amount of Slaked Lime Ca(OH)2​.		”Temp = Bicarbonate = Boiling”
Easily removed just by boiling or adding lime.
Permanent HardnessCalcium Chloride CaCl2​, CaSO4​
Magnesium Chloride MgCl2​, MgSO4​		1. Washing Soda Method: Treatment with Na2​CO3​ (CaCl2​+Na2​CO3​→CaCO3​↓+2NaCl).
2. Calgon Process: Using Sodium Hexametaphosphate (Na6​P6​O18​).
3. Ion-Exchange Resin Method: Cation and Anion exchange resins (yields pure demineralized water).		”Perm = Chloride & Sulphate”
Boiling FAILS! Requires chemicals (Washing Soda, Calgon, or Resins).

TIP

Hard Water vs Soft Water Quick Summary:

  • Soft Water: Lathers easily with soap; free from Ca2+/Mg2+ ions. (e.g., Rainwater, distilled water).
  • Hard Water: Does NOT lather easily; forms sticky scum (Ca-soap ppt). Soap is wasted! Synthetic detergents work in both hard and soft water.
  • Heavy Water (D2​O): Deuterium Oxide. Used as a Moderator in nuclear reactors to slow down fast neutrons. (Discovered by Harold Urey).

2. Industrial Materials: Glass, Cement, and Ceramics

A. Glass (Supercooled Liquid)

Glass is an amorphous, non-crystalline inorganic solid. It is considered a supercooled liquid because it does not have a sharp melting point and flows slowly over years.

  • Raw Materials: Silica / Sand (SiO2​), Soda Ash (Na2​CO3​), Limestone (CaCO3​).
  • Annealing: Process of slow, controlled cooling of hot glass to eliminate internal stress and prevent brittleness.
  • Types of Glass & Uses:
    • Pyrex / Borosilicate Glass: Contains Boron Oxide (B2​O3​); high thermal shock resistance. Used for laboratory glassware and ovenware.
    • Flint Glass: Contains Lead Oxide (PbO); high refractive index. Used for optical lenses, prisms, and premium glassware.
    • Soda-Lime Glass: Common window glass and bottles.
    • Crookes Glass: Contains Cerium Oxide (CeO2​); absorbs harmful UV rays. Used in sunglasses and protective spectacles.
  • Glass Coloring Agents:
    • Cobalt Oxide (CoO) → Deep Blue | Cuprous Oxide (Cu2​O) → Red
    • Chromium Oxide (Cr2​O3​) → Green | Ferric Oxide (Fe2​O3​) → Yellow / Brown

B. Portland Cement (Joseph Aspdin, 1824)

Fine grey powder manufactured by calcining a mixture of Limestone (CaCO3​) and Clay at 1400−1500∘C to form Clinker, which is ground with Gypsum.

  • Essential Chemical Composition:
    1. Lime (CaO): 60−67% (Provides strength)
    2. Silica (SiO2​): 17−25% (Provides strength)
    3. Alumina (Al2​O3​): 3−8% (Quick setting property)
    4. Iron Oxide (Fe2​O3​): 0.5−6% (Provides color and hardness)
  • Role of Gypsum (CaSO4​⋅2H2​O): Added (2−3%) during final grinding of clinker to slow down the initial setting time of cement (prevents quick setting when mixed with water).

3. Chemical Fertilizers (N-P-K)

Fertilizers supply essential nutrients (Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium) to soil.

  1. Nitrogenous Fertilizers:
    • Urea (NH2​CONH2​): Contains 46% Nitrogen (highest N-content solid fertilizer). Organic compound synthesized in industry from NH3​ and CO2​.
    • Ammonium Nitrate (NH4​NO3​): Source of nitrogen; highly explosive if contaminated.
    • Calcium Ammonium Nitrate (CAN): Known as Kisan Khad; neutral fertilizer.
  2. Phosphatic Fertilizers:
    • Single Superphosphate (SSP): Manufactured from Rock Phosphate and Sulphuric Acid. Contains Ca(H2​PO4​)2​ and Gypsum.
  3. Potassic Fertilizers:
    • Muriate of Potash / Potassium Chloride (KCl).

4. Explosives & Pyrotechnics

  • Gunpowder (Black Powder): Mixture of Potassium Nitrate (KNO3​∼75%, Saltpetre) + Charcoal (C∼15%) + Sulphur (S∼10%).
    • Role of Sulphur in Gunpowder: Acts as a fuel and lowers the ignition temperature to accelerate combustion speed.
  • Dynamite (Alfred Nobel, 1867): Originally made by absorbing liquid Nitroglycerin into inert porous diatomaceous earth (Kieselguhr) to stabilize it.
  • TNT (Trinitrotoluene): C6​H2​(NO2​)3​CH3​. High explosive produced by nitration of toluene.
  • RDX (Research Department Explosive): Cyclotrimethylenetrinitramine (C3​H6​N6​O6​). Also known as Cyclonite or Hexogen.
  • Airbag Chemistry in Automobiles: Airbags deploy upon collision impact triggered by rapid thermal decomposition of Sodium Azide (NaN3​) to generate massive volume of Nitrogen Gas (N2​↑): 2NaN3​(s)Impact Spark​2Na(s)+3N2​(g)↑

5. Paints, Polymers & Binders

  • Paints Components:
    1. Pigment: Provides color and opacity (e.g., Titanium Dioxide TiO2​ white, Zinc oxide).
    2. Binder / Resin: Forms a continuous dry film holding pigment particles together (e.g., Linseed oil, Alkyd resins, Polyurethane, Acrylics).
    3. Solvent / Thinner: Adjusts viscosity for spraying/brushing (e.g., Turpentine, Mineral spirits).
  • Silicones: Synthetic organosilicon polymers with repeating −Si-O-Si− backbone. Water-repellent, heat-resistant, used as sealants, lubricants, and synthetic rubber.

6. Solved PYQs (NDA / CDS / CAPF)

Q1 (NDA II 2024)

Question: Which one among the following fertilizers is a rich source of Nitrogen? (a) Urea | (b) Single Superphosphate | (c) Muriate of Potash | (d) NPK 10:26:26

  • Answer: (a) Urea (46% N).

Q2 (NDA I 2019)

Question: Which one of the following is used as a binder in paints? (a) Silicones | (b) Titanium dioxide | (c) Polyurethane / Resin | (d) Turpentine

  • Answer: (c) Polyurethane / Resin / Linseed oil.

Q3 (NDA II 2013)

Question: What is the presence of sulphur in gunpowder for? (a) To act as an oxidizing agent | (b) To lower ignition temperature and speed up burning | (c) To absorb moisture | (d) To increase weight

  • Answer: (b) To lower ignition temperature and speed up burning.

Q4 (CAPF 2020)

Question: Airbags in cars work on the principle of a chemical reaction triggered by impact producing which gas? (a) Oxygen | (b) Nitrogen | (c) Hydrogen | (d) Carbon monoxide

  • Answer: (b) Nitrogen (N2​) from Sodium Azide (NaN3​).
