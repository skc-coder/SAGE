Acids, Bases, Salts & Indicators

Exhaustive notes covering Acid-Base theories (Arrhenius, Brønsted-Lowry, Lewis), indicators, pH scale & applications, key chemical salts (Bleaching Powder, Baking Soda, Washing Soda, Plaster of Paris), strength of acids/bases, and real PYQs (NDA/CDS/CAPF).

1. Introduction & Physical Properties

  • Acids:
    • Taste: Sour.
    • Effect on Litmus: Turns blue litmus red.
    • Conductivity: Aqueous solutions conduct electricity due to free H+ / H3​O+ ions.
  • Bases & Alkalis:
    • Taste: Bitter, feel soapy/slippery to touch.
    • Effect on Litmus: Turns red litmus blue.
    • Alkali: Bases that are soluble in water (e.g., NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)2​, Mg(OH)2​).
    • Crucial Rule: All alkalis are bases, but not all bases are alkalis. (e.g., Cu(OH)2​, Fe(OH)3​ are insoluble bases, so they are not alkalis). Alkalis are corrosive in nature.

2. Chemical Theories of Acids and Bases

A. Arrhenius Theory

  • Acid: Substance that releases hydrogen ions (H+ or hydronium H3​O+) when dissolved in water. HCl+H2​O→H3​O++Cl−
  • Base: Substance that releases hydroxide ions (OH−) when dissolved in water. NaOHH2​O​Na++OH−

B. Brønsted-Lowry Theory (Proton Transfer)

  • Acid: Proton (H+) donor.
  • Base: Proton (H+) acceptor.
  • Conjugate Acid-Base Pairs: BaseNH3​​​+AcidH2​O​​⇌Conjugate AcidNH4+​​​+Conjugate BaseOH−​​
    • When a base accepts H+, it forms its conjugate acid.
    • When an acid donates H+, it leaves behind its conjugate base.

C. Lewis Theory (Electron Pair Transfer)

  • Lewis Acid: Electron-pair acceptor (Electron-deficient species, electrophiles, vacant orbitals).
    • Examples: BF3​, AlCl3​, AlF3​, BH3​, H+.
  • Lewis Base: Electron-pair donor (Electron-rich species, nucleophiles, at least 1 lone pair).
    • Examples: NH3​, H2​O, OH−, F−, Cl−.
Lewis Acid (e- acceptor, empty orbital)  +  Lewis Base (e- donor, lone pair)
             BF3                           +              :NH3
                                           -->  F3B <- NH3  (Coordinate Bond)

4. Chemical Reactions of Acids and Bases

1. Acid + Metal → Salt + Hydrogen Gas (H2​↑)

Zn+H2​SO4​→ZnSO4​+H2​↑

  • Test for H2​ Gas: Brings a burning candle near the gas bubbles; it burns with a characteristic “POP” sound.

2. Base + Metal → Salt + Hydrogen Gas (H2​↑)

Only certain metals (like Zn, Al) react with strong bases. 2NaOH+Zn→Sodium ZincateNa2​ZnO2​​​+H2​↑

3. Acid + Metal Carbonate / Hydrogen Carbonate → Salt + Water + CO2​↑

Na2​CO3​+2HCl→2NaCl+H2​O+CO2​↑ NaHCO3​+HCl→NaCl+H2​O+CO2​↑

  • Test for CO2​ Gas: Pass gas into lime water (Ca(OH)2​). Lime water turns milky due to insoluble CaCO3​ precipitate: Ca(OH)2​+CO2​→White ppt (Milky)CaCO3​↓​​+H2​O
  • Excess CO2​ Passing: Milkiness disappears because soluble calcium hydrogen carbonate is formed: CaCO3​+H2​O+CO2​→Soluble in waterCa(HCO3​)2​​​

4. Neutralization Reaction (Acid + Base → Salt + Water)

NaOH+HCl→NaCl+H2​O

  • Why Curd/Sour items shouldn’t be kept in Copper/Brass vessels: Sour food contains acids (lactic acid) which react with copper/brass to produce poisonous copper salts.

5. Metallic Oxide + Acid → Salt + Water

  • Metallic oxides are BASIC in nature (e.g., CuO, MgO, CaO). CuO+2HCl→Blue-Green colorCuCl2​​​+H2​O

6. Non-Metallic Oxide + Base → Salt + Water

  • Non-metallic oxides are ACIDIC in nature (e.g., CO2​, SO2​, NO2​). Ca(OH)2​+CO2​→CaCO3​+H2​O
  • Neutral Oxides: CO, NO, N2​O, H2​O (do not react with acid or base).

5. Dilution & Safety Precautions

  • Dilution: Mixing an acid or base with water reduces the concentration of ions (H3​O+ or OH−) per unit volume.
  • CRITICAL SAFETY RULE: Always add Acid to Water slowly with constant stirring. NEVER add Water to Concentrated Acid!
    • Reason: Dissolving acid in water is highly exothermic. Adding water to acid causes localized boiling, splashing of concentrated acid (causing chemical burns), and may fracture the glass container due to excessive local heating.

6. The pH Scale & Real-Life Applications

The pH scale (developed by Sørensen; p stands for German potenz = power) measures hydrogen ion concentration (0 to 14).

pH=−log10​[H+]=log10​[H+]1​

  • pH<7⟹ Acidic solution ([H+]>[OH−])
  • pH=7⟹ Neutral solution ([H+]=[OH−])
  • pH>7⟹ Basic / Alkaline solution ([H+]<[OH−])

D. Everyday Applications & Memory Tricks for pH

  1. How to Remember pH Order Questions:
    • Rule: Strong Acids (0−2) < Weak Acids (3−6) < Neutral (7) < Mild Bases (7.4−10) < Strong Bases (11−14).
    • Exam Solving Trick (SSC / CDS / NDA):
      • Lemon Juice (pH≈2.2) → Strongly Acidic (Lowest pH)
      • Coffee (pH≈5.0) → Weakly Acidic
      • Blood (pH≈7.4) → Slightly Alkaline / Basic
      • Milk of Magnesia (pH≈10.0) → Basic Antacid (Highest pH)
      • Correct Ascending Order: Lemon Juice<Coffee<Blood<Milk of Magnesia.
  2. Human Body pH Range: Works within a narrow range of 7.0−7.8.
  3. Acid Rain: Rainwater with pH<5.6. Caused by atmospheric pollutants (SO2​,NO2​,CO2​) dissolving in rain to form H2​SO4​ and HNO3​. When it flows into rivers, it lowers water pH, killing aquatic life.
  4. Venus Atmosphere: Atmosphere of Venus is composed of thick white and yellowish clouds of Concentrated Sulphuric Acid (H2​SO4​) and 95−97%CO2​.
  5. Tooth Decay: Starts when mouth pH<5.5. Tooth enamel is made of Calcium Phosphate / Hydroxyapatite (Ca5​(PO4​)3​OH), the hardest substance in the human body. Bacteria degrade sugar to produce acid. Toothpaste (which is basic) neutralizes excess acid to prevent decay.
  6. Soil pH Treatment:
    • Acidic Soil (pH<6): Treated by adding Quicklime (CaO), Slaked Lime (Ca(OH)2​), or Chalk (CaCO3​).
    • Basic Soil (pH>8.3): Treated by adding organic matter/manure which releases acids.
  7. Self-Defense by Plants & Animals:
    • Ant stings / Nettle leaves inject Methanoic Acid (HCOOH).
    • Remedy: Rubbing mild basic baking soda or applying Dock plant leaf (nature’s remedy growing near nettles).

7. Demystifying Sodium Carbonates & Soda Terms

IMPORTANT

What the heck is “Soda Ash” vs “Washing Soda” vs “Baking Soda”?

  • Soda Ash: Anhydrous Sodium Carbonate (Na2​CO3​) — Pure carbonate powder without any water of crystallization.
  • Washing Soda: Hydrated Sodium Carbonate (Na2​CO3​⋅10H2​O) — Soda Ash recrystallized with 10 water molecules.
  • Baking Soda: Sodium Bicarbonate / Sodium Hydrogen Carbonate (NaHCO3​) — The acidic salt containing Hydrogen.
  • Caustic Soda: Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH) — The strong alkali base.
