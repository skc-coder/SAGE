# Classification of Matter & Physical States


## 1. Thermal & Physical Phenomena

- **Extinguishing Fire with Water**
    
      
    - **Boiling Water ($100^\circ\text{C}$):** Extinguishes fires faster than cold water, hot water, or ice.
        
          
        
    - **Immediate Latent Heat Absorption:** Already at boiling point, so it instantly absorbs the latent heat of vaporization ($\approx 2260 \text{ kJ/kg}$) upon contact with fire.
        
          
        
    - **Oxygen Deprivation:** Rapidly expands by approximately 1,700 times its volume into steam, displacing oxygen and smothering the flame.
        
          
        
- **Ideal Gas Behavior**
    
      
    - **Speed Distribution:** Gas molecules do not all move at the same speed due to continuous random collisions. Speeds follow a Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution.
        
          
        
    - **Kinetic Energy:** Varies across individual molecules ($E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$).
        
          
        
    - **Potential Energy & Forces:** Ideal gas molecules exert no interactive forces; their potential energy is zero.
        
          
        

## 2. Classification of Matter

| **Feature**    | **Element**                                                                   | **Compound**                                                            | **Mixture**                                                         |
| -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Definition** | Purest, basic form of matter; cannot be broken down physically or chemically. | Formed by 2 or more elements chemically combined in a fixed proportion. | 2 or more substances physically mixed together with no fixed ratio. |
| **Properties** | Retains elemental properties.                                                 | Properties are completely different from constituent elements.          | Retains properties of its constituent substances.                   |
| **Separation** | N/A                                                                           | Chemical or electrochemical reactions only.                             | Physical separation methods (filtration, handpicking, etc.).        |
| **Examples**   | Copper ($\text{Cu}$), Mercury ($\text{Hg}$), Bromine ($\text{Br}$)            | Water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$), Sodium Hydroxide ($\text{NaOH}$)          | Air, Soil, Salt + Water, $\text{Fe} + \text{Cu}$                    |

- **Element Milestones & States**
    
      
    - **Term Coined:** Robert Boyle (1661).
        
          
        
    - **Experimental Definition:** Antoine Lavoisier (1743).
        
          
        
    - **Liquids at Room Temperature:** Mercury ($\text{Hg}$, metal) and Bromine ($\text{Br}$, non-metal).
        
          
        
    - **Liquids Just Above Room Temp ($> 303\text{ K}$):** Gallium ($\text{Ga}$) and Caesium ($\text{Cs}$).
        
          
        
    - **Gaseous Elements:** 11 elements exist naturally as gases at room temperature.
        
          
        

## 3. Solutions, Suspensions, and Colloids

### Key Definitions

- **Solution:** Homogeneous mixture with uniform composition throughout ($\text{Solution} = \text{Solute} + \text{Solvent}$).
    
      
    
- **Tincture of Iodine:** Antiseptic solution containing 2–3% Iodine (solute) dissolved in alcohol and water (solvent).
    
      
    
- **Alcoholic Extract:** Solution made using alcohol as a solvent to draw out active chemical compounds.
    
      
    
- **Mass Concentration Formula:**
    
      
    
    $$\text{Mass \%} = \left(\frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Total Mass of Solution}}\right) \times 100$$
    

### Comparison Matrix

| **Property**       | **True Solution**     | **Colloidal Solution**                                | **Suspension**                        |
| ------------------ | --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **Type**           | Homogeneous           | Heterogeneous (looks homogeneous)                     | Heterogeneous                         |
| **Particle Size**  | $< 1\text{ nm}$       | $1\text{ nm}$ to $1000\text{ nm}$                     | $> 1000\text{ nm}$                    |
| **Visibility**     | Invisible             | Invisible to naked eye; visible under ultramicroscope | Visible with naked eye                |
| **Tyndall Effect** | No scattering         | Scatters light (path visible)                         | Scatters light until particles settle |
| **Stability**      | Very stable           | Stable                                                | Unstable (particles settle)           |
| **Separation**     | Cannot filter         | Centrifugation                                        | Ordinary filtration / Decantation     |
| **Examples**       | Sugar in water, Brass | Milk, Ink, Blood                                      | Sand in water                         |

### Types of Colloids

| **Dispersed Phase (DP)** | **Dispersion Medium (DM)** | **Type**  | **Examples**                      |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------- | --------- | --------------------------------- |
| Liquid                   | Gas                        | Aerosol   | Fog, clouds, mist                 |
| Solid                    | Gas                        | Aerosol   | Smoke, automobile exhaust         |
| Gas                      | Liquid                     | Foam      | Shaving cream                     |
| Liquid                   | Liquid                     | Emulsion  | Milk, face cream                  |
| Solid                    | Liquid                     | Sol       | Milk of magnesia, mud             |
| Gas                      | Solid                      | Foam      | Foam rubber, sponge, pumice stone |
| Liquid                   | Solid                      | Gel       | Jelly, cheese, butter             |
| Solid                    | Solid                      | Solid Sol | Coloured gemstones, milky glass   |

- **Shaving Cream Note:** Classed as a **Foam** in its final dispensed state because pressurized gas forms micro-bubbles inside a liquid soap matrix.
    
      
    

## 4. Separation Methods

- **Sieving:** Separates solid particles of different sizes using a mesh/screen.
    
      
    
- **Winnowing:** Separates solids based on density/weight differences using air currents (e.g., grain from husk).
    
      
    
- **Filtration:** Separates an insoluble solid from a liquid using a porous filter.
    
      
    
- **Separating Funnel:** Separates immiscible liquids based on density differences (e.g., oil and water).
    
      
    
- **Sublimation:** Separates a sublimable solid (solid $\rightarrow$ gas directly) from a non-sublimable solid (e.g., salt + camphor or ammonium chloride).
    
      
    
- **Crystallisation:** Purifies solids by forming pure crystals from a saturated solution (preferred over evaporation as it avoids charring).
    
      
    
- **Distillation:** Used for miscible liquids with a large boiling point difference ($\ge 25\text{ K}$, e.g., acetone + water).
    
      
    
- **Fractional Distillation:** Used for miscible liquids with a small boiling point difference ($< 25\text{ K}$). Employs a fractionating column filled with glass beads for repeated condensation-evaporation cycles (e.g., petroleum refining, air separation).
    
      
    
- **Chromatography:** Separates solutes based on differing solubilities in a mobile phase moving across a stationary phase (e.g., ink dyes, drugs in blood).
    
      
    
- **Centrifugation:** Rotates mixtures at high speed to push denser particles to the bottom and lighter ones to the top (e.g., separating cream from milk).
    
      
    

## 5. Water Purification & Chemical Reactions

### Municipal Water Treatment Flow

1. **Reservoir:** Raw water storage.
    
      
    
2. **Sedimentation Tank:** Suspended heavy solids settle by gravity.
    
      
    
3. **Loading Tank:** Alum added to aggregate fine colloidal particles.
    
      
    
4. **Filtration Tank:** Sand and gravel layers filter remaining impurities.
    
      
    
5. **Chlorination:** Germicides/chlorine added to kill bacteria.
    
      
    
6. **Ion-Exchange Method:** Uses cation ($H^+$) and anion ($OH^-$) resins to yield demineralized water.
    
      
    

### Physical vs. Chemical Changes

|**Property**|**Physical Change**|**Chemical Change**|
|---|---|---|
|**Substance**|No new substance formed.|One or more new substances formed.|
|**Reversibility**|Usually reversible.|Irreversible.|
|**Examples**|Phase changes, cutting trees, melting butter|Burning oil, rusting iron, browning apple, neutralization|

- **Burning Candle:** Exhibits both physical change (melting of wax) and chemical change (burning of the wick and vaporized wax releasing $\text{CO}_2$ and soot).

**Explanation:**

  

- **Centrifugation** spins a mixture rapidly, using centrifugal force to pull very fine, suspended insoluble particles to the bottom so they can be separated from the liquid.
    
      
    
- **Crystallization** is used to purify soluble solids from a solution.
    
      
    
- **Fractional distillation** is used to separate miscible liquids with different boiling points.
    
      
    
- **Decantation** works for separating larger, heavier insoluble solids that settle quickly on their own, but fails for very small particles suspended in liquid.

**(a) Sulphur in carbon disulphide**

  

**Explanation:**

  

- **Sulphur in carbon disulphide** forms a homogeneous solution (true solution) because sulphur dissolves completely in carbon disulphide ($\text{CS}_2$), making its composition uniform throughout. Therefore, it is **NOT** a heterogeneous mixture.
    
      
    
- **Mixture of sugar and salt crystals** is a heterogeneous mixture because individual particles of sugar and salt remain distinct.
    
      
    
- **Sandy water** is a heterogeneous mixture (suspension) as sand does not dissolve and settles at the bottom.
    
      
    
- **Sulphur in water** is a heterogeneous mixture because sulphur is insoluble in water.
