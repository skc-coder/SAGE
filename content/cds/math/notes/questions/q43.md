# CDS Practice Q43: Complementary Angles Tower Height

## Problem Statement

The angles of elevation of the top of a tower from two points which are at distances of $10\text{ m}$ and $5\text{ m}$ from the base of the tower and in the same straight line with it are complementary. What is the height of the tower?

- (a) $5\text{ m}$
- (b) $15\text{ m}$
- (c) $\sqrt{50}\text{ m} = 5\sqrt{2}\text{ m}$
- (d) $75\text{ m}$

---

## Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Identify Applicable Subtopic Theorem
Refer to [[content/cds/math/notes/subtopics/height_complementary_theorem|Complementary Angles Height Theorem]].

When angles of elevation from two points on the same side at distances $a$ and $b$ are complementary ($a = 10\text{ m}$, $b = 5\text{ m}$):

$$h = \sqrt{ab}$$

### Step 2: Direct Substitution & Calculation
Substitute $a = 10$ and $b = 5$:

$$h = \sqrt{10 \times 5} = \sqrt{50}\text{ m}$$

Simplifying the radical:

$$h = \sqrt{25 \times 2} = 5\sqrt{2}\text{ m}$$

---

## Final Answer
- **Correct Option**: **(c) $\sqrt{50}\text{ m}$**
