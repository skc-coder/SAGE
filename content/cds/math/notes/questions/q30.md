# Q30

## Question Statement

If $k$ is any even positive integer, then $(k^2 + 2k)$ is:

(a) divisible by 24  
(b) divisible by 8 but may not be divisible by 24  
(c) divisible by 4 but may not be divisible by 8  
(d) divisible by 2 but may not be divisible by 4  

## Solution

### Method 1: Exam Value Substitution (Fastest ⚡)

Since $k$ is an **even positive integer**:

1. **Test $k = 2$**:
   $$k^2 + 2k = 2^2 + 2(2) = 4 + 4 = 8$$
   - Divisible by 8 and 4, but **NOT** divisible by 24 ($8 / 24$ is not an integer).
   - This eliminates option (a).

2. **Test $k = 4$**:
   $$k^2 + 2k = 4^2 + 2(4) = 16 + 8 = 24$$
   - Divisible by 8 and 24.

3. **Test $k = 6$**:
   $$k^2 + 2k = 6^2 + 2(6) = 36 + 12 = 48$$
   - Divisible by 8 and 24.

4. **Test $k = 8$**:
   $$k^2 + 2k = 8^2 + 2(8) = 64 + 16 = 80$$
   - Divisible by 8, but **NOT** divisible by 24 ($80 / 24 = 3.33$).

**Conclusion from testing**:
$(k^2 + 2k)$ is **always divisible by 8**, but **may not be divisible by 24** (e.g. for $k=2$ and $k=8$).

### Method 2: Algebraic Derivation

Let $k = 2m$ (where $m$ is any positive integer):
$$k^2 + 2k = (2m)^2 + 2(2m) = 4m^2 + 4m = 4m(m + 1)$$

Since $m$ and $m+1$ are consecutive integers, one of them is always **even** (divisible by 2):
- $m(m+1)$ is always divisible by 2 $\implies m(m+1) = 2p$.

Therefore:
$$k^2 + 2k = 4 \times 2p = 8p$$

Thus, $(k^2 + 2k)$ is **guaranteed to be divisible by 8**. However, it is divisible by 24 only when $3 \mid m(m+1)$, which does not hold for all $m$ (e.g., $m=1 \implies k=2$).

**Correct Answer:** **(c) divisible by 4 but may not be divisible by 8** $\rightarrow$ **Wait, let's re-verify 8**:
Since $4m(m+1) = 8p$, it is **ALWAYS** divisible by 8! So option (c) is wrong, option (b) is **CORRECT**!

**Correct Answer:** **(b) divisible by 8 but may not be divisible by 24**
