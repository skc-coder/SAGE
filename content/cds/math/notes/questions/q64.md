# Question 64: Bounded Range Optimization in 3-Set Venn Diagram

## Question
In a gathering of 100 people, 70 can speak Hindi, 60 can speak English, and 30 can speak French. Further, 30 can speak both Hindi and English, 20 can speak both Hindi and French. If $x$ is the number of people who can speak both English and French, then which one of the following is correct? (Assume everyone speaks at least one language).
- (a) $10 < x \le 30$
- (b) $0 \le x < 8$
- (c) $x = 9$
- (d) $x = 8$

---

## Detailed Solution

### Step 1: Write Inclusion-Exclusion Data
Let $H, E, F$ be the sets of people speaking Hindi, English, and French:
- $n(U) = n(H \cup E \cup F) = 100$
- $n(H) = 70, \quad n(E) = 60, \quad n(F) = 30$
- $n(H \cap E) = 30, \quad n(H \cap F) = 20, \quad n(E \cap F) = x$
- Let $g = n(H \cap E \cap F)$ be the number of people speaking all three languages.

### Step 2: Apply 3-Set Formula
$$n(H \cup E \cup F) = n(H) + n(E) + n(F) - [n(H \cap E) + n(H \cap F) + n(E \cap F)] + n(H \cap E \cap F)$$

Substitute the known values:
$$100 = 70 + 60 + 30 - (30 + 20 + x) + g$$
$$100 = 160 - 50 - x + g$$
$$100 = 110 - x + g \implies x - g = 10 \implies g = x - 10$$

### Step 3: Apply Venn Diagram Region Non-Negativity Constraints
All regional counts in a Venn diagram must be $\ge 0$:
1. $g \ge 0 \implies x - 10 \ge 0 \implies x \ge 10$.
2. Only $(E \cap F) = x - g = 10 \ge 0$ (verified).
3. Total speaking French $n(F) = 30$. Since $n(E \cap F) = x \le n(F)$, we must have:
   $$x \le 30$$

Therefore, combining the bounds:
$$10 \le x \le 30$$

Since $x - g = 10 \implies g = x - 10$, for $g > 0$, $x > 10$.
Hence $10 < x \le 30$.

---

## Key Takeaway & Pattern
- When solving 3-variable Venn diagram inequality problems, express the triple intersection $g = n(A \cap B \cap C)$ in terms of the unknown variable $x$.
- Use the non-negativity condition $g \ge 0$ for the lower bound and $x \le \min(n(E), n(F))$ for the upper bound!
