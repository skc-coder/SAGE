# CDS Practice Q46: Shortest Perpendicular Distance of Road to Tower

## Problem Statement

A person is at point $C$ and observes a tower in West direction at $B$ at a distance of $200\text{ m}$. He walks along a straight road to point $A$ where he observes the tower in South direction at $B$ at a distance of $300\text{ m}$. What is the shortest perpendicular distance of the tower from the road?

- (a) $300\text{ m}$
- (b) $600/\sqrt{13}\text{ m}$
- (c) $500/\sqrt{13}\text{ m}$
- (d) $200\text{ m}$

---

## Detailed Step-by-Step Solution

### Step 1: Identify Geometry of Horizontal Plane
Refer to [[content/cds/math/notes/subtopics/height_3d_perpendicular_distance|3D Orthogonal Road Distance & Bearings]].

- Foot of tower is at $B$.
- Point $C$ is East of $B \implies BC = 200\text{ m}$.
- Point $A$ is North of $B \implies AB = 300\text{ m}$.
- Since West/East and North/South are perpendicular directions, $\angle ABC = 90^\circ$.

### Step 2: Hypotenuse Road Distance ($AC$)
In right-angled triangle $\Delta ABC$:

$$AC = \sqrt{AB^2 + BC^2} = \sqrt{300^2 + 200^2} = \sqrt{90000 + 40000} = \sqrt{130000} = 100\sqrt{13}\text{ m}$$

### Step 3: Compute Perpendicular Altitude ($BD$)
Let $BD = p$ be the shortest perpendicular distance from $B$ to hypotenuse road $AC$.
Equating area of $\Delta ABC$:

$$\text{Area} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot AB \cdot BC = \frac{1}{2} \cdot AC \cdot p$$

$$300 \times 200 = 100\sqrt{13} \times p$$

$$60000 = 100\sqrt{13} \cdot p \implies p = \frac{60000}{100\sqrt{13}} = \frac{600}{\sqrt{13}}\text{ m}$$

---

## Final Answer
- **Correct Option**: **(b) $\frac{600}{\sqrt{13}}\text{ m}$**
