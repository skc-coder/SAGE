# Question 118: Harmonic Progression Interchanged Terms Invariant

If the 7th term of a Harmonic Progression (HP) is 1/10 and the 10th term is 1/7, then what is its 17th term?

(a) $\frac{1}{17}$  
(b) $\frac{17}{70}$  
(c) $\frac{1}{70}$  
(d) $\frac{1}{27}$  

> [!faq]- View Solution
> **Shortcut Invariant:**  
> If $m$-th term of HP is $\frac{1}{n}$ and $n$-th term of HP is $\frac{1}{m}$, then:
> - Reciprocal AP has $m$-th term $= n$ and $n$-th term $= m$.
> - For reciprocal AP: $a + (m-1)d = n$ and $a + (n-1)d = m \implies d = 1, a = 1$.
> - Thus, the $k$-th term of the reciprocal AP is $T_k = k$.
> - Therefore, the $k$-th term of HP is $\frac{1}{k}$.
> - Specifically, the $(m+n)$-th term of HP is $\frac{1}{m+n}$.
> 
> **Step-by-Step Derivation:**  
> - $m = 7$, $n = 10$.
> - We need the $(7+10) = 17$th term of HP.
> - Using the invariant: $T_{17} = \frac{1}{17}$.
> 
> **Correct Answer:** **(a) $\frac{1}{17}$**
