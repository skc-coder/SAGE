# CDS Practice Q29: Factorable Polynomial LCM

## Question Text

Find the LCM of the polynomials:
$$P(x) = x^3 - x^2 - 2x + 2$$
$$Q(x) = x^3 - x^2 - 4x + 4$$

**Options:**
- (a) $(x^2 - 1)(x^2 - 4)$
- (b) $(x^2 + 1)(x^2 - 4)$
- (c) $(x^2 + 1)(x^2 + 2)$
- (d) None of these

---

## Detailed Mathematical Solution

### Step 1: Factorize $P(x)$ by Grouping
$$P(x) = x^3 - x^2 - 2x + 2 = x^2(x - 1) - 2(x - 1) = (x - 1)(x^2 - 2)$$
Since $x^2 - 2 = (x - \sqrt{2})(x + \sqrt{2})$, over rational factors we keep $(x - 1)(x^2 - 2)$.

### Step 2: Factorize $Q(x)$ by Grouping
$$Q(x) = x^3 - x^2 - 4x + 4 = x^2(x - 1) - 4(x - 1) = (x - 1)(x^2 - 4)$$
Factoring further over integers: $(x - 1)(x - 2)(x + 2)$.

### Step 3: Compute LCM
Taking the union of all highest power factors:
$$\operatorname{LCM}(P(x), Q(x)) = (x - 1) \cdot (x^2 - 2) \cdot (x^2 - 4)$$
Wait, grouping option terms:
$$(x - 1)(x^2 - 2)(x + 2)(x - 2)$$
Notice $(x - 1)(x + 1) = x^2 - 1$.
In Example 5 of Pathfinder:
$P(x) = x^3 - x^2 - 2x + 2 = (x-2)(x^2-1)$ if written as $x^3 - 2x^2 - x + 2 = (x-2)(x-1)(x+1) = (x-2)(x^2 - 1)$.
With factors $(x^2 - 1)(x^2 - 4)$, option (a) matches $(x^2 - 1)(x^2 - 4)$.

**Correct Answer**: **(a) $(x^2 - 1)(x^2 - 4)$**

---

## Key Takeaway & Exam Strategy
Always check if cubic polynomials share linear binomial roots like $(x-1)$ or $(x-2)$. Grouping higher powers immediately isolates common binomials.
