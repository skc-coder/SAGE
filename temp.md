# National Income
#economics #cds #gk

---

## What is National Income?

**Income** = reward for work done.

**National Income** = final monetary value of all goods and services *produced* in a country within a year.

> Why "final"? Because we only count the end product, not intermediate stages. If steel goes into a car, counting both the steel and the car would be *double counting*.

---

## What is NOT counted in NI

| Excluded item             | Reason                                               |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| Illegal goods/services    | Not legally sanctioned production                    |
| Non-economic activities   | Done out of love/satisfaction, not reward            |
| Intermediary goods        | Counted in final product already (anti double-count) |
| Housewives' contribution  | No market wage assigned                              |
| Share market transactions | Asset transfer, not production                       |
| Pension                   | Transfer payment, not factor income                  |
| Second-hand goods         | Already counted when first produced                  |
| Savings/stock             | Not production of new goods/services                 |

> **Maid vs. Mother example:** A maid cleaning your house *is* counted (paid wage = economic activity). Your mother doing the same task is *not* counted (non-economic). Same physical work, different economic treatment — because one has a market price, the other doesn't. This is a well-known critique of NI measurement.

## Circular Flow of Income

Two sectors: **Households** (factors of production) ↔ **Business Houses** (goods/services)

```
Households ──[Land, Labour, Capital, Entrepreneur]──► Business
Households ◄──[Rent, Wages, Interest, Profit]──────── Business
Households ──[Payment for goods/services]──────────► Business
Households ◄──[Goods and Services]─────────────────── Business
```

This gives us the **three methods** to calculate NI:

---

## Methods of Calculating NI

### 1. Income Method
$$NI = R + W + I + P$$
(Rent + Wages + Interest + Profit)

### 2. Expenditure Method
$$NI = C + G + I + (X - M)$$
(Private Consumption + Govt Expenditure + Investment + Net Exports)

> **Officially used in India** to calculate NI.

### 3. Value Added / Product Method
Aggregate contribution of each sector (Primary + Secondary + Tertiary).
Does **not** include second-hand goods (already produced, no new value added).

## Key Concepts and Formulas

### Gross vs Net
$$Gross - Depreciation = Net$$
$$Net + Depreciation = Gross$$

> **Depreciation** = wear and tear on capital goods over time. A bike worth ₹15L today may be worth ₹13L after 2 years — the ₹2L lost is depreciation.

---

### Market Price vs Factor Cost
$$MP = FC + Indirect\ Tax - Subsidy$$
$$FC = MP - Indirect\ Tax + Subsidy$$

> Example: Water bottle costs ₹500 to make (FC). ₹100 GST added, ₹10 subsidy given → Market Price = ₹590.
> 
> Caveat: GDP at MP *includes* indirect taxes but *excludes* subsidies — Q4 DPP caught this. GDP at FC strips all that out to get the actual factor rewards.

---

### Domestic vs National
$$Domestic + NFIA = National$$
$$National - NFIA = Domestic$$

**NFIA = Net Factor Income from Abroad**

| Component | Sign |
|---|---|
| Income earned by Indians abroad | + |
| Income earned by foreigners in India | − |

> Why does this matter? India's domestic boundary includes land area + 200 NM EEZ (as per UNCLOS) + military establishments + Indian embassies abroad. An Indian working in the US contributes to India's *National* income but not *Domestic* income.
> 
> Note: US Embassy in Delhi = US territory → NOT part of India's NI.

---

### The NI Family Tree

```
GDP  (Gross Domestic Product)
 │  − Depreciation
 ▼
NDP  (Net Domestic Product)
 │  + NFIA
 ▼
NNP  (Net National Product)
 │  + Depreciation
 ▲
GNP  (Gross National Product)

NNP at Factor Cost = Official National Income of India
Base year: 2011-12
```

> **Exam trap:** GNP − NNP = Depreciation. NDP − GDP = also Depreciation. Always check if you're moving domestic↔national or gross↔net.

---

### Nominal vs Real GDP

| | Nominal GDP | Real GDP |
|---|---|---|
| Prices used | Current year prices | Base year prices (2011–12) |
| What it reflects | Inflated economy | Actual output growth |

$$GDP\ Deflator = \frac{Nominal\ GDP}{Real\ GDP} \times 100$$

> GDP Deflator is released by **NSO**. It measures average price change across the *entire* economy — broader than CPI or WPI which track specific baskets.

---

### Personal Income and Disposable Income

$$PI = NI - Undistributed\ Profit - Corporate\ Tax - Net\ Interest\ Payment + Transfer\ Payment$$

$$Personal\ Disposable\ Income = PI - Personal\ Tax$$

---

### Per Capita Income (PCI)

$$PCI = \frac{Total\ Income}{Total\ Population}$$

> Better welfare indicator than GDP because it adjusts for population size. A country can have high GDP but poor PCI if population is huge (classic India vs smaller nations comparison).
> 
> **Kuznets Curve** → graphs PCI against inequality. As PCI rises, inequality first rises then falls (inverted U shape) — named after Simon Kuznets.

---

### Other Ratios

- **Tax-to-GDP ratio** → higher ratio = more govt capacity to spend on infrastructure
- **Debt-to-GDP ratio** → measures fiscal health

---

## Human Development Index (HDI) — brief

Part of Human Development Report. Key indices:
- **HDI** — Human Development Index
- **GII** — Gender Inequality Index
- **MPI** — Multidimensional Poverty Index
  - MPI dimensions: Health, Education, Standard of Living
  - Employment status is **NOT** a Standard of Living indicator under MPI — common trap question

---

## Growth Rate Formula

$$Growth\ (\%) = \frac{New - Old}{Old} \times 100$$

Example: NI was 50 in 2011–12, now 75 → Growth = (25/50) × 100 = **50%**

## Practice Questions (integrated)

**Q: Participation of states in policy formulation was absent in?**  
→ **(D) Planning Commission** — NITI Aayog has a Governing Council with states. Planning Commission didn't.

**Q: Which is NOT a factor of production?**  
→ **(D) Wage** — Wage is a factor *payment*, not a factor of production. Factors are Land, Labour, Capital, Entrepreneur.

**Q: NDP + NFIA = ?**  
→ **NNP** (Net National Product)

**Q: NNP + Depreciation = ?**  
→ **GNP**

**Q: GNP − NNP = ?**  
→ **Depreciation**

**Q: GDP at Market Price includes indirect taxes but excludes subsidies — True or False?**  
→ **True** (DPP Q4, Statement 1 only correct)

**Q: What does the GDP Deflator measure?**  
→ Avg change in prices in the overall economy; released by NSO; covers all goods + services.

**Q: Imputed rent is included in NI because?**  
→ It is a **productive service** — owner-occupied houses provide housing services whether or not rent is paid.

**Q: US Embassy in Delhi — part of India's NI?**  
→ **No** — it is US territory. Indian Embassy in the US *is* part of India's territory for NI purposes.

**Q: Which graph reflects PCI and inequality?**  
→ **Kuznets curve**

**Q: What is NOT included under Standard of Living in MPI?**  
→ **Employment status**
