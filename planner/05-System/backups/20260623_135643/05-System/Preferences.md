# Preferences & Working Style

## Chronotype
- Peak energy hours: 6–10 AM, 2–5 PM (post-lunch slump around 1–2 PM)
- Lowest energy hours: Late evening (after 7 PM), early mornings before coffee/breakfast (4–6 AM)

## Planning style that has failed before
- Crammed all mocks into final 10-20 days instead of spacing them through the year
- Handwritten notes too messy to revise from — made them unusable later
- Studied passively without practicing problems — low retention, low confidence in exams
- Attempted full syllabus revision right before exam → triggered panic attack, brain fog, lost confidence on exam day
- "Study without measuring time" → sprawling 12–14 hour days with no breaks → brain slowdown → compulsive studying → crash

## What actually works for me
- **Structured 2-hour study blocks** with fixed breaks (15 min minimum between blocks), not open-ended grinding
- **Daily practice problems** mixed into study (not theory-only)
- **Mocks spaced weekly** from week 5 onward, not crammed at the end
- **Fixed meal times OFF the desk** — forces actual breaks
- **Fixed sleep time (9 PM)** — non-negotiable
- **Daily movement** (walk, run) — prevents the mental/physical collapse that happens with 14-hour desk days
- **Sunday rest day** with light review + show/film + human contact — prevents panic spiral

## Motivators / what makes me follow through
- Just passed 4th year CSE (2026) — building a stable future is the main driver
- GATE is the real shot; AFCAT/CDS are genuine backups (not anxiety-adding extra exams)
- Fear of another panic attack like 2026 → keeps me honest about rest/fitness/structure
- Seeing progress on GATE mocks (not just "studied more hours") → concrete motivation

## Known patterns
- **Gets stuck and lethargic on tasks outside comfort zone** — tasks stall silently for weeks (e.g. AFCAT form filled, syllabus untouched for weeks before this session)
- **Gets distracted by Reddit/YouTube during study sessions**, especially content about politics/world manipulation — recognizes it's a problem but feels unable to stop ("can't help"). This distraction acts as an **escape hatch when studying gets hard**; willpower doesn't hold under stress
- **Nervous that GATE might not work out** → tries to add CDS, DSSSB, "more exams" as backups → this is anxiety, not strategy, and leads to scattered prep and burnout
- **Zero outside time, no movement, isolated studying during prep → nervous system stays high-alert → more distraction/avoidance/YouTube spiral**
- **Last year: endless studying without rest, breaks, or fitness → brain got slow → panic attack on exam day → lost confidence mid-exam**
- **Studies long hours but skips rest/movement/social time entirely during prep** — directly linked to last exam's panic attack and mental fatigue
- **Wakes at 5 AM, feels sleepy for 3–4 consecutive days** → will shift sleep to 9 PM to make this sustainable; monitor in week 2
- DSSSB added June 2026 -- user states this is necessary, not anxiety-driven (counter-note to backup-exam pattern above)
